Each question gives two people's defenses of the same choice for one step. Pick the sound one.

The Harbor Science Museum hosts school field trips, and most of its schools come back every year.
Its bookings coordinator runs this workflow every week, for about 150 booking requests a week from
teachers.

1. Collect the week's booking requests from the form on the museum's website. (a program)
2. Read each request and pull out the school, the date wanted, the number of students and the
   program asked for. (who?)
3. Check each pulled-out request against the teacher's own words, fix any mistakes, and approve
   the week's bookings. (the bookings coordinator)
4. Reserve a room and a guide for each approved booking. (a program)
5. Decide each teacher's complaint about a guide: no action, a talk with the guide, or a written
   warning, and tell the guide. (who?)

**About step 2:** Its input is about 150 requests a week, typed in teachers' own words, and many
returning teachers ask only for "the usual" without naming a program. A mistake here is caught at
step 3, where the bookings coordinator checks every pulled-out request against the teacher's own
words. Some requests name students with a food allergy, so the lunchroom can plan. The week's
requests are all in by Thursday at noon.

**About step 5:** Its input is about three complaints a week from teachers about something a guide
said or did, and whether the guide was at fault is a judgment no form settles. A wrong decision
can cost a guide their job or leave a real problem in place, and it goes to the guide with no one
checking it. The complaints name the guide and the students involved. The education director
handles these on Thursday afternoons. Teachers send complaints in the days after their visit.

### q1

Nadia and Ben both think step 2 should be done by an AI agent rather than a person. Here is how
each puts it.

**Defense 1, Nadia's:** An AI agent rather than a person helps timeliness, because pulling the
details out of about 150 requests a week would take a person hours. It hurts correctness, because
the bookings coordinator checks every pulled-out request at step 3.

**Defense 2, Ben's:** An AI agent rather than a person helps timeliness, because pulling the
details out of about 150 requests a week would take a person hours. It hurts correctness, because
an agent can't tell which program a returning teacher means by "the usual".

Which defense is sound?

1. Nadia's
2. Ben's
