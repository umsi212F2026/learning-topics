Pan and Pantry is a recipe site with about 40,000 readers. Its community editor runs this workflow
every week to handle what readers send in: about 600 comments a week, and a few reports from
readers who say a recipe made them ill.

1. Collect the week's new comments, and any reports that a recipe made someone ill, from the
   site's forms. (a program)
2. Read each comment and sort it: post it, pass it to the editor as a question, or reject it as
   spam or abuse. (who?)
3. Go over the sorted comments, move any that are in the wrong pile, and approve the ones to post.
   (the community editor)
4. Post each approved comment under its recipe. (a program)
5. Decide for each illness report whether to keep the recipe up, add a warning to it, or take it
   down, and reply to the reader. (who?)

**About step 2:** Its input is about 600 typed comments a week, from a one-word "yum" to a long
account of a recipe gone wrong. A mistake here is caught at step 3, where the community editor goes
over every sorted comment before any is posted. Each comment carries the reader's full name and
email address, which the site never shows. The week's comments are all in by Sunday at midnight.

**About step 5:** Its input is about three reports a week from readers who say a recipe made them
ill, and whether the recipe itself was unsafe is a judgment no form settles. A wrong call can leave
up a recipe that makes another reader seriously ill, and no one checks the decision afterward. The
reports describe the reader's illness, often with what a doctor found. The site's founder handles
these on Saturday mornings. Reports come in on any day of the week.

## Framing

Each question gives two people's defenses of the same choice for one step.

### q1

Ines and Kofi both think step 2 should be done by an AI agent rather than a person. Here is how
each puts it.

**Defense 1, Ines's:** An AI agent rather than a person helps timeliness, because the week's
comments are all in by Sunday at midnight. It hurts privacy, because readers' full names and email
addresses go to the agent's provider.

**Defense 2, Kofi's:** An AI agent rather than a person helps timeliness, because reading about 600
comments a week would take a person most of a day. It hurts privacy, because readers' full names
and email addresses go to the agent's provider.

Which defense is sound?

1. Ines's
2. Kofi's

### q2

Hana and Victor both think step 5 should be done by a person rather than an AI agent. Here is how
each puts it.

**Defense 1, Hana's:** A person rather than an AI agent helps privacy, because readers' illnesses
and what their doctors found stay out of the agent's provider. It hurts timeliness, because the
founder only gets to these on Saturday mornings.

**Defense 2, Victor's:** A person rather than an AI agent helps privacy, because readers' illnesses
and what their doctors found stay out of the agent's provider. It hurts timeliness, because only
about three illness reports reach the site each week.

Which defense is sound?

1. Hana's
2. Victor's
