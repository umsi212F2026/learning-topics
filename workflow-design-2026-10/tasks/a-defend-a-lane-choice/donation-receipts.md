A nonprofit that runs after-school classes sends every donor a tax receipt and answers donors
who write in about their gifts. About sixty donations come in a normal week, more in December.

1. Collect the week's donations from the donation page and the bank deposits into one list. (a
   program)
2. Check the list against the bank statement and approve it. (the treasurer)
3. Make a tax receipt for each donation on the approved list, mail it, and log it in the donor
   records. (who?)
4. Answer the few donors each week who write asking for a gift back or for it to go to a
   different use. (who?)
5. Update the donor records with whatever was decided. (a program)

In the office manager's words: "The approved list has one line per donation, with the donor's
name, home mailing address and amount. Only two or three donors a week write asking for a gift
back or for it to go somewhere else, and no policy we have settles most of them: a man who paid a
year's pledge up front and has since lost his job, a widow asking us to return the last of her late
husband's monthly gifts. Their letters tend to explain why, so they carry things like a layoff, a
divorce or a death in the family. A few lines on the approved list each week come from donors in
Canada and are marked 'CAD', because the amount is in Canadian dollars. A receipt for the wrong
amount is a tax record: the donor files it with their taxes, we report the same figure, and once
it's in the mail nobody checks it again. If we turn down a request we should have granted, that
donor can be out several hundred dollars they badly need, and our reply goes straight out with no
second reader. Our director answers these on Thursday afternoons, her one day in the office. The
receipts go out in envelopes with a logo the children in our classes drew. The director signs her
replies with the nonprofit's motto, 'Every kid, every afternoon.'"
