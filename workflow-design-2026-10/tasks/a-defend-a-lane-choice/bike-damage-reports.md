A city bike-share keeps its 400 bikes safe by acting on riders' damage reports. Riders send about
200 reports a week through the bike-share's app.

1. Collect the week's damage reports from the app. (a program)
2. Read each report, match it to a bike, and mark it "pull from service" or "check at next visit",
   with a one-line reason. (who?)
3. Go over the marked reports each morning, fix any wrong match or mark, send the mechanic out,
   and sign off a list of the bikes repaired. (the fleet lead)
4. Mark each bike on the signed-off list as available to rent in the fleet system. (who?)
5. Send the city the week's repair totals. (a program)

In the fleet lead's words: "Riders type the reports on their phones, anything from 'brakes???' to
a whole paragraph, and many describe the bike by where it's parked rather than by its number,
though two of our docks are both called Main Street. The signed-off list, usually sixty or so bikes
a week, has one line per bike with its number and the part replaced, and nothing on it is about
riders. A lot of riders add their full name and phone number to a report and ask us to call them
back. I go over every marked report each morning, and a wrong match or mark almost never gets past
me. A few lines a week cover two bikes, because the mechanic fixed a pair together and wrote both
numbers on one line. The app's report button turned green last year. Once a bike shows as
available, it goes straight back to its dock for rent, and if it still has a fault, the next we
hear of it is from a rider who has been hurt. The fleet system came secondhand from a neighboring
city's bike-share."
