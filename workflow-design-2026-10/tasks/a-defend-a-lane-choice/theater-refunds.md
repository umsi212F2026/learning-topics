patrons who can't come to a show they have paid for. In a busy week, well over a hundred refund
requests come in.

1. Collect the week's refund requests from the form on the theater's website. (a program)
2. Read each refund request and sort it into refund, exchange or not eligible under the theater's
   refund policy, with a one-line reason. (who?)
3. Go over the sorted requests, move any that are in the wrong pile, and approve the list of
   refunds with an amount for each. (the box office manager)
4. Send each refund on the approved list back to the card the tickets were bought with. (who?)
5. Email each patron what was decided about their request. (a program)

In the manager's words: "The request form still carries the heading the old manager gave it,
'Sorry you can't make it!' The requests are typed in patrons' own words, some a single line and
some a long story, and many name the show without saying which night, though we run each show on
several nights. Quite a few patrons explain that they had a hospital stay or an illness in the
family, so the requests carry health details. When a request lands in the wrong pile, I nearly
always catch it when I go over the sorted list. The approved list, about eighty refunds in a busy week, has one line per refund, with the
patron's name, the card number the tickets were bought with and the amount, though a few lines a
week have a note where the amount should be, such as 'half, see email'. A refund sent for the
wrong amount comes straight out of the theater's account, and once it is on a patron's card
nobody looks at it again. We started giving refunds back to cards, rather than by check, two
seasons ago."
