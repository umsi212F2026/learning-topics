A tutoring center matches children with tutors at the start of every term. In the first weeks of a
term, about 120 requests a week come in from parents.

1. Collect the week's requests from the center's sign-up page. (a program)
2. Read each request and propose a tutor for it from the tutor list, by subject and free hours,
   with a one-line reason. (who?)
3. Go over the proposed matches, fix any that are wrong, and set aside any request that asks for
   something the tutor list can't settle. (the coordinator)
4. For each set-aside request, choose the tutor, or decide the center can't take the child this
   term. (who?)
5. Email each family and tutor their match. (a program)

In the coordinator's words: "Parents type their requests in their own words, some a few lines and
some a whole page about their child, and they often list the hours their child can't do beside
the hours they can, so a 'not Tuesdays' is easy to read as a Tuesday. Because many families want
tutoring at home, most requests carry the family's home address. Only three or four requests a
week get set aside, and they are the ones no form settles: a boy who refused to work with his last
two tutors, sisters three grades apart who want to be tutored together. I go over every proposed
match before anything goes out, and I catch nearly every wrong one. The sign-up page still has the
center's old orange logo. The set-aside requests often explain the family's custody arrangement,
such as which parent the child is with on which days. If one of those children ends up with the
wrong tutor, the family has usually prepaid a term of about six hundred dollars, and nobody looks
at a match again once the email goes out. Our director chooses these on Saturday mornings, after
the week's sessions are done. She is training for a marathon this fall."
