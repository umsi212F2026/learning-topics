The circulation manager of a small public library runs this workflow every week to follow up on
overdue books. About 120 books are overdue in a typical week.

1. Gather this week's overdue loans from the checkout records into one list, each with the book,
   the borrower and the due date. (a program)
2. Read the messages borrowers sent this week about their overdue books, match each message to
   the loan it is about, and list the matched messages under each loan, then decide for each of
   those loans whether to waive its fine, such as for a book lost in a house fire or one the
   borrower says they already returned. (an AI agent, then the circulation manager)
3. Work out each borrower's fine at 10 cents a day for each overdue book, leaving out the fines
   that were waived. (a program)
4. List each borrower's overdue books with the fine on each, look up from their library account
   how the borrower prefers to be reached, by email or at a mailing address, and write each
   borrower a short, friendly reminder that names their books and fine and says how to return
   them. (an AI agent)
5. Send each borrower their reminder the way they prefer to be reached. (a program)

Some of these steps do more than one step's work.

### q1

Step 2 does too much for one step. Where inside it should a step boundary go, and why there? What
would be saved at that boundary?

### q2
