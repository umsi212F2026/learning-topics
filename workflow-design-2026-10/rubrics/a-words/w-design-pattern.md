# Rubric: Design pattern

If the learner misses a question here, set a DEFINE or INTERPRET move for design pattern live, as
help (it is recorded as helped and doesn't count), then come back to a production question.

A design pattern is a named set of design choices that are often made together, because they have
worked well together in many past workflows. Three things the word carries: it is a set of choices,
not one choice; it is a way of arranging the work, not a goal the work aims at (an intermediate goal
such as failing loudly is what a design aims at, and a pattern is one way of getting there); and it
recurs, which is why it is worth a name, but it is not a rule: a workflow may use it, use part of
it, or not use it, depending on whether the reasons it worked elsewhere hold here. "Pattern" alone
means the same and is not a confusable. This word has no nearest confusable, so its bank has no
DISTINGUISH question.

### catch-must-save

- **goal:** `w-design-pattern`
- **move:** CATCH
- **answer:** Being a design pattern doesn't make Checkpoints required. A pattern is a set of
  choices that often go together because they have suited many workflows; whether it suits this
  one depends on whether its reasons hold here, and the owner may save some steps' outputs (the
  agent's draft, say) and not others.
- **credit:** full for naming that calling Checkpoints a design pattern says it is a commonly used
  combination, not a rule, so it doesn't oblige this workflow to save every step's output; saying
  which outputs are worth saving here is welcome but not required. Half for saying they needn't
  save every output, with a reason about this workflow or about cost (more to save, name and keep
  in sync), without saying why the pattern's being a pattern doesn't oblige them. None for a
  different quibble alone, such as where the outputs should be saved or that the owner's review
  should come at a different point.
- **tutor note:** a learner who argues that saving every output is a good idea here has said
  something about this workflow, not about the word; ask whether "it's a design pattern" would be
  a reason to do it even if it weren't a good idea.

### catch-pick

- **goal:** `w-design-pattern`
- **move:** CATCH
- **type:** mcq
- **answer:** 3
- **credit:** full for 3 only. Choice 3 names a set of choices often made together (an agent
  writes down what should change, a person approves, a program makes the change), which is the
  propose-apply pattern. Choice 1 treats a pattern as a rule; maker-checker suits an unreliable
  step, and the program's step behaves the same every time, so nothing obliges a check after it.
  Choice 2 is one choice, who sends the reminders, not a set of choices. Choice 4 names an
  intermediate goal, something the design aims at, not the choices that get there; the program's
  flagging is one way the library reaches it.
- **tutor note:** a learner who picks 4 may be counting anything the reading named in bold as a
  pattern; ask whether failing loudly says how the work is arranged or what the arrangement should
  achieve.
