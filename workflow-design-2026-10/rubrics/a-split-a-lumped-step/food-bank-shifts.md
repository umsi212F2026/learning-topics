Steps 2 and 4 are the lumped ones; steps 1, 3 and 5 are single acts. Both lumped steps are
Medium: the join with no change in each lies between two parts that are plainly one kind of act.

Step 2 is `check-split`, done by the agent throughout, with its join with no change first. Its
three parts are assigning volunteers to shifts, listing the assignments by shift, and writing
first-time volunteers' notes. The assignments and the list by shift are both checked against the
sign-ups and the staffing rule: every volunteer is on a shift they said they can work, and no
shift has fewer than three people. The notes are checked by a person reading them for warmth and
clarity. So the only boundary goes before the notes.

Step 4 is `handoff-split`, with its change join first. Its three parts are the coordinator's
decisions on which absences to excuse, the program's count of each volunteer's unexcused absences,
and the program's list of everyone at three or more. The excuse decisions need the judgment of
someone who knows the volunteers and the food bank's way of treating absences; the count and the
list are both checked against those decisions, by recounting. So the only boundary goes at the
handoff from the coordinator to the program.

### q1

- **goal:** `c-divide-into-steps`
- **cases:** same-executor
- **answer:** The boundary goes after the assignments are listed by shift and before the notes are
  written, not between assigning and listing, since the agent does both and both are checked the
  same way. It goes there because the kind of check changes: the assignments are checked against
  the sign-ups and the rule of at least three people a shift, while the notes need a person to read
  them for tone and clarity. Save the list of assignments by shift, each volunteer with their
  shift, so it can be checked against the sign-ups before any note is written.
- **credit:** full when the answer puts the boundary between listing the assignments and writing
  the notes; gives a reason naming the two kinds of check, each said even briefly (such as "check
  against the sign-ups" or "count each shift" for one, "read for tone" for the other); and names a
  saved result concrete enough to check the assignments before the notes are written. The list of
  assignments by shift passes, and so do the assignments themselves, since listing them by shift
  only re-sorts them. The sign-up list, a count or summary ("how many volunteers per shift"), or
  the notes do not. Half when the boundary is at that join with only one of the other two: a
  reason that is missing, generic ("smaller steps are easier"), or resting only on an executor
  change the answer proposes (having a person check the notes), which is not in this step; or a
  saved result that is missing or fails that test. At most half for an answer that also puts a
  boundary between assigning and listing, since nothing changes there, so that boundary has no
  reason. None when the only boundary is between assigning and listing, or no boundary is placed.
- **tutor note:** if they say the boundary goes there "so a person can check the notes", ask how
  the assignments before that point would be checked, and how that differs from how the notes
  would be. If they put a boundary between assigning and listing, ask how each of those two
  results would be checked.

### q2

- **goal:** `c-divide-into-steps`
- **cases:** executor-changes
- **answer:** The boundary goes at the handoff, after the coordinator decides which absences to
  excuse and before the program counts them, not between counting and listing, since the program
  does both and both are checked against the coordinator's decisions. It goes there because the
  executor changes from the coordinator to the program, and the check changes with it: the excuse
  decisions need judgment from someone who knows the volunteers, while the counts can be checked
  against those decisions by recounting. Save the coordinator's decisions, each missed shift marked
  excused or unexcused, so a volunteer wrongly on the list can be traced to a decision or to the
  count.
- **credit:** full when the answer puts the boundary at the handoff between the coordinator's
  excuse decisions and the program's count; gives a reason naming the change of executor, or the
  change in the kind of check, or both; and names a saved result concrete enough to check the
  excuse decisions before the counting runs. The coordinator's decisions on each missed shift pass.
  The shift leads' reports of who missed a shift, a count or summary ("how many absences were
  excused"), or the final list of volunteers at three or more do not. Half when the boundary is at
  the handoff with only one of the other two: a reason that is missing or generic ("smaller steps
  are easier"), or a saved result that is missing or fails that test. At most half for an answer
  that also puts a boundary between counting and listing, since nothing changes there, so that
  boundary has no reason. None when the only boundary is between counting and listing, or no
  boundary is placed.
- **tutor note:** if they save the shift leads' reports, ask whether those reports would show
  which absences the coordinator excused.
