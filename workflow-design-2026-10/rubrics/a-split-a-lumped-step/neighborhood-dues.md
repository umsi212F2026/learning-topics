Steps 2 and 5 are the lumped ones; steps 1, 3 and 4 are single acts. Step 2 is Hard and step 5
is Medium.

Step 2 is `check-split`, done by the agent throughout, with its change join first and the
judgment side first. Its three parts are writing the letter, making a copy addressed to each
household, and printing a label for each envelope. The letter is checked by a person reading it
for whether it is friendly, clear and fair to what the money paid for. The addressed copies and
the labels look like different kinds of thing, but both are checked the same way, against the
list of member households: every household once, with its name and address right. So the only
boundary goes after the letter is written. This is the Hard join: a learner may take the move from
letters to labels as a change in the kind of check, though nothing changes there.

Step 5 is `handoff-split`, with its join with no change first. Its three parts are the program's
list of households that have not paid, the program's sorting of that list by street, and the
treasurer's decisions on whom to visit. The list and the sorted list are plainly one kind of act,
both checked against the payment records. The decisions need the judgment of someone who knows
the neighbors. So the only boundary goes at the handoff from the program to the treasurer.

### q1

- **goal:** `c-divide-into-steps`
- **cases:** same-executor
- **answer:** The boundary goes after the letter is written and before the addressed copies are
  made, not between the copies and the labels, since the agent does both and both are checked
  against the list of member households. It goes there because the kind of check changes: the
  letter needs a person to read it for tone and clarity, while the copies and labels are checked
  against the household list, every household once with the right name and address. Save the
  letter's text, so it can be read and approved before a hundred and fifty copies are made from it.
- **credit:** full when the answer puts the boundary between writing the letter and making the
  addressed copies; gives a reason naming the two kinds of check, each said even briefly (such as
  "read for tone" for one, "check against the member list" for the other); and names a saved
  result concrete enough to check the letter before any copy is made. The letter's text passes.
  The treasurer's spending notes, the household list, a summary of what the letter says, or the
  addressed copies or labels do not. Half when the boundary is at that join with only one of the
  other two: a reason that is missing, generic ("smaller steps are easier"), or resting only on an
  executor change the answer proposes (having the treasurer approve the letter), which is not in
  this step; or a saved result that is missing or fails that test. At most half for an answer that
  also puts a boundary between making the copies and printing the labels, since nothing changes
  there, so that boundary has no reason. None when the only boundary is between the copies and the
  labels, or no boundary is placed.
- **tutor note:** if they put a boundary before the labels because "labels are a different thing
  from letters", ask what someone would compare each one against to find a mistake.

### q2

- **goal:** `c-divide-into-steps`
- **cases:** executor-changes
- **answer:** The boundary goes at the handoff, after the program sorts the unpaid households by
  street and before the treasurer decides whom to visit, not between listing and sorting, since
  the program does both and both are checked against the payment records. It goes there because
  the executor changes from the program to the treasurer, and the check changes with it: the list
  can be checked against the payment records, while the decisions need judgment from someone who
  knows the neighbors. Save the list of households that have not paid, sorted by street, so a
  household wrongly on it can be found before anyone knocks on a door.
- **credit:** full when the answer puts the boundary at the handoff between the program's sorted
  list and the treasurer's decisions; gives a reason naming the change of executor, or the change
  in the kind of check, or both; and names a saved result concrete enough to check the list
  before the treasurer decides. The sorted list of unpaid households passes, and so does the list
  before sorting, since sorting by street only re-orders it. The payment records, a count or
  summary ("how many households haven't paid"), or the treasurer's decisions do not. Half when the
  boundary is at the handoff with only one of the other two: a reason that is missing or generic
  ("smaller steps are easier"), or a saved result that is missing or fails that test. At most half
  for an answer that also puts a boundary between listing and sorting, since nothing changes
  there, so that boundary has no reason. None when the only boundary is between listing and
  sorting, or no boundary is placed.
- **tutor note:** if they save only the payment records, ask whether those would show a mistake in
  which households the program listed.
