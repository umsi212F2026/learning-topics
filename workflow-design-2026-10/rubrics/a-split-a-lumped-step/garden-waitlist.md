Steps 2 and 4 are the lumped ones; steps 1, 3 and 5 are single acts. Step 2 is Hard and step 4
is Medium.

Step 2 is `handoff-split`, with its change join first. Its three parts are the coordinator's
decisions on which households to pass over, the program's match of each open plot to the
longest-waiting household not passed over, and the program's offer emails. The pass-over
decisions need the judgment of someone who knows the households and what their notes mean. The
matches and the emails look like different kinds of thing, but both are checked the same way, by
the same program, against the waitlist and the pass-over decisions: each open plot goes to the
household that has waited longest and was not passed over, and each email carries that
household's name and plot. So the only boundary goes at the handoff from the coordinator to the
program. This is the Hard join: a learner may take the move from matching to writing emails as a
change in the kind of check, though nothing changes there.

Step 4 is `check-split`, done by the agent throughout, with its change join first and the
judgment side first. Its three parts are writing the welcomes, setting each under its plot number,
and putting them in plot-number order. The welcomes are checked by a person reading them for
warmth and clarity. Setting them under plot numbers and ordering them are plainly one kind of act,
both checked against the list of households that accepted: every new gardener appears once, under
the plot they accepted, in order. So the only boundary goes after the welcomes are written.

### q1

- **goal:** `c-divide-into-steps`
- **cases:** executor-changes
- **answer:** The boundary goes at the handoff, after the coordinator decides which households to
  pass over and before the program matches plots to households, not between matching and filling
  in the emails, since the program does both and both are checked against the waitlist and the
  pass-over decisions. It goes there because the executor changes from the coordinator to the
  program, and the check changes with it: the pass-over decisions need judgment from someone who
  knows the households, while the matches can be checked against the waitlist order. Save the
  coordinator's list of households passed over this month, so a household that missed a plot can
  be traced to a decision or to the matching.
- **credit:** full when the answer puts the boundary at the handoff between the coordinator's
  pass-over decisions and the program's matching; gives a reason naming the change of executor,
  or the change in the kind of check, or both; and names a saved result concrete enough to check
  the pass-over decisions before the matching runs. The coordinator's list of households passed
  over, or the decision on each household's note, passes. The households' notes, a count or
  summary ("how many households were passed over"), the matches or the offer emails do not. Half
  when the boundary is at the handoff with only one of the other two: a reason that is missing or
  generic ("smaller steps are easier"), or a saved result that is missing or fails that test. At
  most half for an answer that also puts a boundary between matching and filling in the emails,
  since nothing changes there, so that boundary has no reason. None when the only boundary is
  between matching and filling in the emails, or no boundary is placed.
- **tutor note:** if they put a boundary before the emails because "writing an email is a
  different job", ask how a wrong email would be found, and how a wrong match would be.

### q2

- **goal:** `c-divide-into-steps`
- **cases:** same-executor
- **answer:** The boundary goes after the welcomes are written and before they are set under plot
  numbers and ordered, not between setting them under plot numbers and ordering them, since the
  agent does both and both are checked against the list of households that accepted. It goes
  there because the kind of check changes: the welcomes need a person to read them for warmth and
  clarity, while the arranging is checked against the accepted offers, each new gardener once,
  under the right plot, in order. Save the welcomes, each with the gardener's name, so they can be
  read before the newsletter section is put together.
- **credit:** full when the answer puts the boundary between writing the welcomes and setting them
  under plot numbers; gives a reason naming the two kinds of check, each said even briefly (such
  as "read for warmth" for one, "check against who accepted" or "check the plot numbers" for the
  other); and names a saved result concrete enough to check the welcomes before they are arranged.
  The welcomes, each tied to its gardener, pass. The waitlist forms, the list of households that
  accepted, a count or summary ("how many welcomes were written"), or the finished newsletter
  section do not. Half when the boundary is at that join with only one of the other two: a reason
  that is missing, generic ("smaller steps are easier"), or resting only on an executor change the
  answer proposes (having a person check the welcomes), which is not in this step; or a saved
  result that is missing or fails that test. At most half for an answer that also puts a boundary
  between setting the welcomes under plot numbers and ordering them, since nothing changes there,
  so that boundary has no reason. None when the only boundary is between those two, or no boundary
  is placed.
- **tutor note:** if they say the boundary goes there "so a person can read the welcomes", ask how
  the arranging after that point would be checked, and how that differs.
