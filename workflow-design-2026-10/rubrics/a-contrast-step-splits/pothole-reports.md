Steps 2 and 3 are the lumped ones. Step 2 is `check-split`: the same agent does all three parts.
Merging the reports and sorting the merged potholes by district are both checked against the
week's reports (every report lands in exactly one entry, each entry in its right district), so
there is no boundary between them. The descriptions need a person to read them for clarity, so
the boundary goes before the descriptions. Step 3 is `handoff-split`: the supervisor's choice is a
judgment call, checked by someone who knows the roads, while the program's routes and texts are
both checked against the supervisor's list (every chosen pothole on one route, every resident told
the right day). Its join with no change is between the routes and the texts.
