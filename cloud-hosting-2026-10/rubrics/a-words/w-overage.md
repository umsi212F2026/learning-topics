# Rubric: overage

If the learner misses a question here, set a DEFINE or INTERPRET move for overage live, as help (it
is recorded as helped and doesn't count), then come back to a production question.

An overage is use past what your plan includes, and the charge for it. The confusable is hitting a
limit that pauses the app, where use past the limit stops the app instead of being charged for.
"Overage charges" means the same as overage and is not a confusable.

### q1

- **goal:** `w-overage`
- **move:** DISTINGUISH
- **answer:** With overages, the app keeps running past 100 GB and you are charged for the extra,
  so a sudden flood of traffic can mean a large bill. With a pausing limit, the app stops at
  100 GB and you pay nothing more, but nobody can use it until the limit resets or you upgrade.
  One risks money, the other risks the app being down.
- **credit:** full for the difference that matters: an overage keeps the app up and bills you for
  the extra; a pausing limit stops the app and bills nothing. Half for one side only. None for an
  incidental difference alone, such as which plan costs more each month.

### q2

- **goal:** `w-overage`
- **move:** CATCH
- **answer:** $7 is the plan's own price. An overage is only the extra charged for use beyond what
  the plan includes, so it isn't a fixed monthly amount: it is nothing in a month that stays inside
  the plan, and could be more than $7 in a busy one.
- **credit:** full for naming that the plan's price is not an overage, which is the charge for use
  past what the plan includes. Half for "that's just the plan price" without saying what an
  overage is instead. None for a different quibble alone, such as that $7 is too much for a class
  project.
