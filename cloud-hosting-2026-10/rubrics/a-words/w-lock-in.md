# Rubric: lock-in

If the learner misses a question here, set a DEFINE or INTERPRET move for lock-in live, as help (it
is recorded as helped and doesn't count), then come back to a production question.

Lock-in is what it would cost you to move to another vendor: mostly work, such as rewriting what
depends on the vendor's own tools and converting data out of its formats. The confusable is a
contract, an agreement that binds you to stay or pay for a term. "Vendor lock-in" means the same as
lock-in and is not a confusable. Copperstack is made up.

### q1

- **goal:** `w-lock-in`
- **move:** DISTINGUISH
- **answer:** A contract is an agreement you made to stay or pay for a set time. Lock-in is what
  moving off the vendor would cost you, in rewriting what depends on its own tools and getting your
  data out, whatever any agreement says. You can be badly locked in with no contract at all, and
  under contract with almost no lock-in.
- **credit:** full for the difference that matters: a contract is a promise to stay or pay, while
  lock-in is the cost of moving, and either can exist without the other. Half for one side only.
  None for an incidental difference alone, such as that contracts are legal documents or that
  lock-in is worse.

### q2

- **goal:** `w-lock-in`
- **move:** CATCH
- **answer:** Lock-in is the cost of moving, and that is mostly work, not the plan's price. A free
  plan can still lock you in: moving off Copperstack would mean replacing its build configuration and
  converting the files out of its own format.
- **credit:** full for naming that lock-in is measured by what moving would take, not by what the
  plan costs, and pointing to the Copperstack-specific configuration or storage as that cost. Half for
  "free doesn't mean no lock-in" without saying what the cost of moving is here. None for a
  different quibble alone, such as that Copperstack might start charging.
