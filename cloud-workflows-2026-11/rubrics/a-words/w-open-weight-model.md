# Rubric: Open-weight model

If the learner misses a question here, set a DEFINE or INTERPRET move for open-weight model live, as help (it is recorded as helped and doesn't count), then come back to a production question.

An open-weight model is one whose trained weights, the numbers that make up the model, are published, so anyone may download it and run it on hardware of their own choosing. Whoever runs it pays for that hardware, so open-weight does not mean free to run, and once it runs on someone's own server the company that published it sees nothing sent to it. Open-source model and open model are other names for it. A free tier is a different thing: what a vendor lets you use without paying, up to a limit, while the model stays on the vendor's servers and the vendor sees every request. The best open-weight models are less capable than the best ones model companies sell.

### vs-free-tier

- **goal:** `w-open-weight-model`
- **move:** DISTINGUISH
- **type:** mcq
- **answer:** 2
- **credit:** full for 2 only. Company B publishes the model's numbers for anyone to download and run, which is an open-weight model. Company A lets the club use its model at no charge up to 50 requests a day while the model stays on Company A's servers, which is a free tier. Choice 1 reads open-weight as free to use. Choice 3 counts the free tier as open-weight too. Choice 4 misses that Company B publishes its model's weights.
- **tutor note:** a learner who calls Company A's offer open-weight may be reading "open" as "open to use at no charge"; ask where the model runs under each offer, and who could run Company A's model on their own computer.

### catch-pick

- **goal:** `w-open-weight-model`
- **move:** CATCH
- **type:** mcq
- **answer:** 2
- **credit:** full for 2 only. The club runs the published model on its own rented server, so the notes go to that server and no model company sees them. Choice 1 treats an open-weight model as one still run by its publisher, which is what calling a model company's model would be. Choice 3 gets the term backwards: an open-weight model is one anyone may download and run elsewhere, which is what the club did. Choice 4 stretches the term past what it names: open-weight says the weights are published, not how capable the model is, and the best open-weight models are less capable than the best ones model companies sell.

### catch-pick-2

- **goal:** `w-open-weight-model`
- **move:** CATCH
- **type:** mcq
- **answer:** 4
- **credit:** full for 4 only. An open-weight model's weights are published, so the clinic may download the model and run it on the server it rents.
