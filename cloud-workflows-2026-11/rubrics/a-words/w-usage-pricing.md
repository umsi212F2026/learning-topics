# Rubric: Usage-based pricing

If the learner misses a question here, set a DEFINE or INTERPRET move for usage-based pricing live, as help (it is recorded as helped and doesn't count), then come back to a production question.

Usage-based pricing is paying according to how much is used, such as per token or per run, rather than a fixed amount each month. Pay-as-you-go and metered billing are other names for it. The bill grows with use because each unit adds to it, not because the price per unit rises. A subscription is the contrast: a fixed amount each month however much is used, up to its limits. An overage is different again: use past what a plan includes, and the charge for it, so it needs a plan with an included amount to go past; a bill that grows with use under a plan with no limit is not an overage.

### vs-subscription

- **goal:** `w-usage-pricing`
- **move:** DISTINGUISH
- **type:** mcq
- **answer:** 1
- **credit:** full for 1 only. Plan A charges by the page, so what the user pays follows how much they translate. Plan B is a subscription: a fixed $15 each month however many pages, up to its limit. Choice 2 reads the monthly plan as usage-based because it counts pages toward a limit. Choice 3 counts both. Choice 4 misses that Plan A charges per page.
- **tutor note:** a learner who picks 2 or 3 may be reading the 2,000-page limit as paying for use; ask what a user who translates 10 pages pays under each plan, and what one who translates 1,000 pays.

### catch-pick

- **goal:** `w-usage-pricing`
- **move:** CATCH
- **type:** mcq
- **answer:** 4
- **credit:** full for 4 only. The club pays for each million tokens, so twice the runs used about twice the tokens and cost about twice as much. Choice 1 reads the doubled bill as an overage, but the billing has no monthly limit to go past. Choice 2 reads usage-based pricing as a subscription, a fixed amount each month. Choice 3 stretches the term: the price per million tokens is set, and the bill grew because more tokens were used, not because each cost more.
