Steps 2 and 3 are the lumped ones. Step 2 is `handoff-split`: the agent's marks and groups are both
checked against the requests and the market's permit rules (every vendor with a current permit
allowed, every allowed vendor in its right group), so there is no boundary between them. The
manager's choice of vendors is a judgment call, checked by someone who knows the market, so the
boundary goes at the handoff. Step 3 is `check-split`: the same agent does all three parts, and
here the change comes first. Whether the layout gives each row a good mix needs a person's
judgment; the alphabetical list and the fees are both checked against the layout and the price
table, so there is no boundary between them, though they produce things that look different.
