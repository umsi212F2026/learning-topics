A farmers market has 30 stalls and fills them each week from the vendors who ask, with a weekly
workflow. About 50 vendors ask for a stall in a typical week.

1. Collect the week's stall requests from the vendors' online form into one list. (a program)
2. Check each vendor's permit date against the market's rules and mark the request allowed or not,
   list the allowed vendors grouped by what they sell, then choose which of them get this week's
   30 stalls, so the market has a good mix. (an AI agent, then the market manager)
3. Draft a layout of the market that spreads the chosen vendors along the rows so each row has a
   good mix, list each vendor's stall number in alphabetical order, and work out each vendor's
   stall fee from the market's price table for that stall. (an AI agent)
4. Email each vendor their stall number and fee. (a program)

Some of these steps do more than one step's work.

### q1

Zara, Adam and Bea each propose splitting step 2 into two steps.

1. Zara: split after the agent groups the allowed vendors, and keep the week's stall requests from
   step 1.
2. Adam: split after the agent groups the allowed vendors, and keep a count of the allowed vendors
   in each group.
3. Bea: split after the agent groups the allowed vendors, and keep that grouped list of allowed
   vendors.

Which proposal is the best division of step 2?

1. Zara's
2. Adam's
3. Bea's

### q2

Caleb, Dina and Emil each propose splitting step 3 into two steps.

1. Caleb: split after the agent drafts the layout, and keep that layout, each row with its stalls
   and vendors.
2. Dina: split after the agent lists each vendor's stall number, and keep that alphabetical list of
   vendors and stall numbers.
3. Emil: split after the agent drafts the layout, and pass it straight on to the next part without
   storing it.

Which proposal is the best division of step 3?

1. Caleb's
2. Dina's
3. Emil's
