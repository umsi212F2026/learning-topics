# Rubric: Fail loudly

If the learner misses a question here, set a DEFINE or INTERPRET move for fail loudly live, as help (it is recorded as helped and doesn't count), then come back to a production question.

A step fails loudly when, if something goes wrong, it reports the problem where a person will see it, when they will see it. What counts is whether the report reaches someone, not how it looks: a note in a log nobody reads, or a warning on a screen nobody watches, is not failing loudly. Failing loudly doesn't have to mean stopping; a step can carry on with the other items and put the bad one on a list the person reads. Fail fast is the course's contrast: stopping at the first sign of trouble. The two are separate. A step can fail fast and still fail silently, if it stops and nobody looks, and a step can fail loudly without stopping at all.

### vs-fail-fast

- **goal:** `w-fail-loudly`
- **move:** DISTINGUISH
- **type:** compound
- **tutor note:** the copying step fails fast but not loudly: it stops at the first photo that won't copy, and the only report is a log file nothing sends Rosa to. The captioning step fails loudly but not fast: it carries on, and the skipped photo reaches Rosa in an email she reads. A learner who calls the copying step both is reading stopping as being loud; ask how Rosa would find out the backup stopped. One who calls the captioning step neither may think failing loudly means stopping; ask whether Rosa learns about the unreadable photo.

### catch-pick

- **goal:** `w-fail-loudly`
- **move:** CATCH
- **type:** mcq
- **answer:** 2
- **credit:** full for 2 only. The program's only report is a note in a folder nothing sends Sam to (he looks over the training sheet, not that folder), so the problem never reaches a person: it does not fail loudly. Choice 1 reads fail loudly as its confusable: stopping at once is failing fast, and this step stops and still says nothing Sam will see. Choice 3 stretches the word to any written note; a note nobody reads is not a report a person will see. Choice 4 takes stopping as part of failing loudly; a step can carry on and still tell Sam about the bad record.

### catch-red-warning

- **goal:** `w-fail-loudly`
- **move:** CATCH
- **type:** mcq
- **answer:** 1
- **credit:** full for 1 only. The warning appears in a terminal window that closes when the run ends, and the treasurer's routine is the Monday list of matched payments, which says nothing about the unmatched one; so nobody sees it. Failing loudly means the problem is reported where a person will see it, and how big or red the warning is doesn't matter if nobody is looking. Choice 2 agrees with the treasurer, taking how striking the warning is for whether it reaches anyone. Choice 3 reads fail loudly as its confusable: stopping is failing fast, and a step can carry on and still fail loudly. Choice 4 is the same mistake as 2 with sound in place of color.
- **tutor note:** a learner who picks 3 is reaching for fail fast; ask whether stopping would tell the treasurer anything if the window still closes before Monday.
