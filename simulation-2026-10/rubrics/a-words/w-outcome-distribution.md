# Rubric: Outcome distribution

If the learner misses a question here, set a DEFINE or INTERPRET move for outcome distribution
live, as help (it is recorded as helped and doesn't count), then come back to a production
question.

An outcome distribution is how often each simulation outcome turns up across many trials with the
same model parameters. The average outcome is one summary of it, a single number that hides the
spread and the rare results. A distribution needs many trials, so one trial broken into parts is
not one; it needs the same parameters throughout, so trials of two settings pooled together are
not one; and it is about what comes out of the trials, not the chance put into the model's rules.

### vs-average

- **goal:** `w-outcome-distribution`
- **move:** DISTINGUISH
- **answer:** The outcome distribution is how often each number of hours turned up across the
  5,000 trials: perhaps no stoppage in many weeks, a few hours in most, and 30 hours or more in a
  rare few. The average, 6 hours, is one number that summarizes it and hides the spread: two very
  different distributions can share it, and it can't say how often a really bad week comes.
- **credit:** full for naming that the distribution says how often each outcome turns up across
  the trials, while the average is a single summary of it that hides the spread or the rare
  results. Half for "the distribution is all the results and the average is one number" without
  saying what the average leaves out. None for an incidental difference alone, such as that the
  average is easier to report.

### catch-one-summer

- **goal:** `w-outcome-distribution`
- **move:** CATCH
- **answer:** The keeper ran one trial, one summer, and its outcome is 7 empty days; splitting that
  summer by month doesn't make a distribution. An outcome distribution is how often each number of
  empty days turns up across many trials with the same settings, such as 0 days in a third of the
  summers and 7 or more in one summer in ten.
- **credit:** full for naming that this is one trial's result, and that an outcome distribution
  needs many trials with the same parameters, showing how often each outcome turns up. Half for
  "you need to run it more times" without saying what the distribution would then show, or for
  saying the monthly breakdown isn't a distribution without saying why. None for a different
  quibble alone, such as that the model's rain is too simple.

### catch-pick

- **goal:** `w-outcome-distribution`
- **move:** CATCH
- **type:** mcq
- **answer:** 1
- **credit:** full for 1 only. How many of the 2,000 months came out at each number of flat days,
  all with the same panel and battery, is the outcome distribution. Choice 2 is the average
  outcome, one summary of the distribution, not the distribution. Choice 3 mixes trials of two
  settings; each battery has its own distribution, to be compared, not pooled. Choice 4 is the
  chance built into the model's rules, an input, not what comes out of the trials.
- **tutor note:** a learner who picks 4 is reading "distribution" as the randomness in the model;
  ask which of the two, the cloudy-day chance or the flat-day counts, the operators could know
  before running anything.
