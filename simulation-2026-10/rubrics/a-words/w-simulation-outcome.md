# Rubric: Simulation outcome

If the learner misses a question here, set a DEFINE or INTERPRET move for simulation outcome live,
as help (it is recorded as helped and doesn't count), then come back to a production question.

A simulation outcome is what a simulation trial produces that the question cares about, such as
who won or how high the water rose. "Output measure" is another name for it, not a confusable. It
belongs to one trial, so it is not the answer to the question, which comes from analyzing many
trials' outcomes together. And it is something a simulated trial produced: not what happened in the
real world, not a result someone hopes for, and not every value a trial happens to draw along the
way.

### vs-answer

- **goal:** `w-simulation-outcome`
- **move:** DISTINGUISH
- **answer:** A simulation outcome is what one trial produces: this simulated crossing arrived
  three days late. The answer to the company's question comes from the outcomes of many trials
  taken together, such as "about 1 crossing in 8 ends more than two days late". One crossing's
  outcome can't say how often anything happens.
- **credit:** full for naming that an outcome belongs to one trial, while the answer comes from
  analyzing the outcomes of many trials together. Half for a difference of form, such as "the
  outcome is a number of days and the answer is a percentage", without saying that one comes from
  a single trial and the other from many. None for an incidental difference alone, such as that the
  answer is what the company acts on.

### catch-real-week

- **goal:** `w-simulation-outcome`
- **move:** CATCH
- **answer:** Selling out on Thursday afternoon is what happened in the real store, not a
  simulation outcome. A simulation outcome is what one simulated trial produces, such as one
  simulated week in which the toy sold out on a Tuesday; the real week is not one of the model's
  trials.
- **credit:** full for naming that the Thursday sell-out happened in the real store, while a
  simulation outcome is what a simulated trial produces. Half for saying the manager has mixed up
  the real week with the model, without saying what a simulation outcome is. None for a different
  quibble alone, such as that one real week can't show whether the model was right.
- **tutor note:** a learner who says "you can't tell from one week whether the model was right"
  is answering a claim the manager didn't make; ask where the Thursday sell-out came from, the
  model or the store.

### catch-pick

- **goal:** `w-simulation-outcome`
- **move:** CATCH
- **type:** mcq
- **answer:** 2
- **credit:** full for 2 only. The number left at closing is what each trial produces that the
  bakery's question cares about, so one trial's count is a simulation outcome. Choice 1 names
  values the trial draws along the way, which the question doesn't ask about. Choice 3 treats one
  trial's outcome as the answer; how many go unsold on a typical Saturday comes from many trials
  together. Choice 4 names a result the bakery hopes for; a simulation outcome is what a trial
  produced, whatever anyone hoped.
- **tutor note:** a learner who picks 3 is close; ask whether a second trial would be bound to
  give 14 as well.
