A stochastic model of a chairlift: each skier's run back down takes a random time around an average one parameter sets, and chairs leave at a fixed spacing. Its four model parameters are the number of skiers on the mountain, the seats on each chair, the time between chairs and the average run time; the setup gives no values. One trial is one day. The model leaves out lift stops, and no question turns on them. Shapes, in served order: q1 `likely-ahead`, q2 `better-worst`, q3 `threshold-worst`, q4 `possible-chance`. Between them the four carry all five cases.

Rule on what the learner says the answer would be about, not on the words they use. A frequency in an answer settles nothing by itself: q2's answer is a pick between two named options that makes a long wait rarer, and q3's is a value for one setting that keeps an outcome to a stated frequency, and neither is "how often, under that change". Only q1 asks for a frequency at one setting. An answer that names two kinds without settling on one gets no credit.

### q1

- **goal:** `c-name-question-kind`
- **cases:** what-if-ahead
- **answer:** What outcomes are likely: Ingrid names one setting for the time between chairs, a chair every 12 seconds, and wants to know how often a skier waits longer than ten minutes at that setting. Phrasings that count: "how often, under that change", "the chance of a long wait with chairs every 12 seconds", "what waits to expect at that setting", "the spread of waits at that one setting". Lure phrasings that don't count: "what would have happened if the lift had run every 12 seconds", "a replay of a past day with faster chairs" (a counterfactual past, pulled by "What if"); and "whether 12 seconds beats the current spacing" (which option, a comparison the question never asks for).
- **credit:** full when the answer says Ingrid wants to know how often an outcome comes about at one named setting, by that label or by saying the answer would be a frequency or distribution at that setting; none when it names another kind, or names this kind and another without settling on this one.
- **tutor note:** if they say "what would have happened", ask whether Ingrid is asking about a day that already took place or one still ahead.

### q2

- **goal:** `c-name-question-kind`
- **cases:** worst-case-mention, chance-in-it
- **answer:** Which option is better: Kofi names two changes, six-seat chairs or chairs closer together, and wants to know which makes half-hour waits rarer. Phrasings that count: "which plan wins", "which of the two ideas cuts long waits more", "a comparison of the two changes to pick one". Lure phrasings that don't count: "how bad the waits can get", "the longest anyone waits" (how bad, pulled by "the worst case"); and "how often skiers wait half an hour" (likely outcomes, pulled by "rarer", with no comparison of the two ideas).
- **credit:** full when the answer says Kofi wants the two named options compared to find which comes out ahead, by that label or by saying the answer would be one of the options; none when it names another kind, or names this kind and another without settling on this one.
- **tutor note:** if they answer "how bad things get", ask what Kofi would be handed back as the answer: a length of wait, or one of his two ideas?

### q3

- **credit:** full when the answer says Hana wants a value for one setting (the time between chairs) that keeps the outcome to the stated frequency, by that label or by saying the answer would be a time between chairs; none when it names another kind, or names this kind and another without settling on this one.
- **tutor note:** if they say "how often skiers are left in line", ask whether Hana wants a percentage back, or a number of seconds between chairs.

### q4

- **goal:** `c-name-question-kind`
