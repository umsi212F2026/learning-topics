A stochastic model of a pharmacy's prescription counter: customers arrive at random, at the rate one parameter sets, and each prescription takes a random time to fill around an average another sets. Its three model parameters are the number of pharmacists on shift, the average fill time and the arrival rate; the setup gives no values. One trial is one day. The model leaves out refills ordered by phone or online, and no question turns on them. Shapes, in served order: q1 `possible-worst`, q2 `likely-ahead`, q3 `how-bad-chance`, q4 `threshold-worst`. Between them the four carry all five cases.

Rule on what the learner says the answer would be about, not on the words they use. A frequency in an answer settles nothing by itself: q3's answer is the most extreme wait over a long stretch, which "rarest" marks out, and q4's is a number of pharmacists that keeps a long wait to a stated frequency; neither is "how often, under that change". Only q2 asks for a frequency at one setting. An answer that names two kinds without settling on one gets no credit.

### q1

- **goal:** `c-name-question-kind`
- **cases:** can-happen, worst-case-mention
- **answer:** Whether something can happen at all: Mei wants to know if the line can reach twenty customers under this model. Phrasings that count: "whether it's possible", "if it can ever occur", "whether the model ever produces a line that long", "a yes or no on whether it could happen". Lure phrasings that don't count: "how bad the line can get", "the longest the line ever gets", "the worst crowd the counter sees" (how bad, pulled by "the worst case").
- **credit:** full when the answer says Mei wants a yes or no on whether that outcome can come about under the model, by that label or by saying what the answer would have to be; none when it names another kind, or names this kind and another without settling on this one.
- **tutor note:** if they say "how long the line can get", ask whether Mei wants a number of customers back, or a yes or no about one length she has in mind.

### q2

- **goal:** `c-name-question-kind`
- **cases:** what-if-ahead
- **answer:** What outcomes are likely: Oskar names one setting, an average fill time of six minutes, and wants to know how often a customer waits over 45 minutes at that setting. Phrasings that count: "how often, under that change", "the chance of a long wait with a six-minute fill time", "what waits to expect with the machine". Lure phrasings that don't count: "what would have happened if the pharmacy had had the machine", "a replay of past days with the machine" (a counterfactual past, pulled by "What if"); and "whether the machine beats the way things run now" (which option, a comparison the question never asks for).
- **credit:** full when the answer says Oskar wants to know how often an outcome comes about at one named setting, by that label or by saying the answer would be a frequency or distribution at that setting; none when it names another kind, or names this kind and another without settling on this one.
- **tutor note:** if they say "whether the machine is worth it", ask whether Oskar mentions any second option to set it against.

### q3

- **goal:** `c-name-question-kind`
- **cases:** chance-in-it
- **answer:** How bad things can get: Yusuf wants the longest waits on the most extreme days over a year, at the current settings. Phrasings that count: "the extreme", "the worst it gets", "the longest wait on the rarest days", "how long the waits get in the tail". Lure phrasings that don't count: "how often customers wait a long time", "the usual wait over a year", "the chance of a busy day" (likely outcomes, pulled by "rarest").
- **credit:** full when the answer says Yusuf wants the most extreme outcome at fixed settings over a long stretch, by that label or by saying the answer would be the longest waits on the rarest days; none when it names another kind, or names this kind and another without settling on this one.
- **tutor note:** if they say "how often waits are long", ask whether Yusuf wants a frequency back, or a length of wait.

### q4

- **goal:** `c-name-question-kind`
- **cases:** target-setting, worst-case-mention, chance-in-it
- **answer:** What setting reaches a target: Leila wants the number of pharmacists on shift at which a two-hour wait happens on no more than 1 day in 50. Phrasings that count: "the setting that gets it there", "how many pharmacists it would take to hit that", "the staffing that keeps two-hour waits that rare". Lure phrasings that don't count: "how bad the waits can get", "the longest a customer waits" (how bad, pulled by "the worst case"); and "how often a customer waits two hours" (likely outcomes, pulled by "1 day in 50").
- **credit:** full when the answer says Leila wants a value for one setting (the number of pharmacists) that keeps the outcome to the stated frequency, by that label or by saying the answer would be a number of pharmacists; none when it names another kind, or names this kind and another without settling on this one.
- **tutor note:** if they say "how often two-hour waits happen", ask what Leila would be handed back as the answer: a percentage, or a number of pharmacists?
