A stochastic model of a wheat field over one season: each day's rain is drawn at random, with the chance one parameter sets, and the crop grows or is set back by the water it gets. Its three model parameters are the chance of rain on a day, the irrigation water given on each dry day and the planting date; the setup gives no values. One trial is one season. The model leaves out pests and plant disease, and no question turns on them. Shapes, in served order: q1 `how-bad-chance`, q2 `threshold-ahead`, q3 `possible-chance`, q4 `better-worst`. Between them the four carry all five cases.

Rule on what the learner says the answer would be about, not on the words they use. A frequency in an answer settles nothing by itself: q1's answer is the most extreme harvest over a long stretch, q2's is an amount of water that makes a harvest level hold in 9 seasons out of 10, and q4's is a pick between two named plans that makes a poor harvest rarer; none of them is "how often, under that change", and no question here asks for a frequency at one setting. An answer that names two kinds without settling on one gets no credit.

### q1

- **goal:** `c-name-question-kind`
- **cases:** chance-in-it
- **answer:** How bad things can get: Anika wants the smallest harvests in the most extreme years over fifty seasons, at the current settings. Phrasings that count: "the extreme", "the worst it gets", "the smallest harvest in the rarest years", "how low the harvest goes in the tail". Lure phrasings that don't count: "how often the harvest is small", "the usual harvest in a dry year", "the chance of a dry year" (likely outcomes, pulled by "rarest").
- **credit:** full when the answer says Anika wants the most extreme outcome at fixed settings over a long stretch, by that label or by saying the answer would be the smallest harvests in the rarest years; none when it names another kind, or names this kind and another without settling on this one.
- **tutor note:** if they say "how often harvests are small", ask whether Anika wants a frequency back, or a number of tonnes.

### q4

- **goal:** `c-name-question-kind`
- **cases:** worst-case-mention, chance-in-it
- **answer:** Which option is better: Nadia names two plans, planting two weeks earlier or more water on each dry day, and wants to know which makes a poor harvest rarer. Lure phrasings that don't count: "how bad the harvest can get", "the smallest harvest in a dry year" (how bad, pulled by "the worst case"); and "how often the harvest falls below half" (likely outcomes, pulled by "rarer" and "usual", with no comparison of the two plans).
- **credit:** full when the answer says Nadia wants the two named options compared to find which comes out ahead, by that label or by saying the answer would be one of the plans; none when it names another kind, or names this kind and another without settling on this one.
- **tutor note:** if they answer "how bad the harvest can get", ask how many plans Nadia mentions and what she wants done with them.
