A stochastic model of a car ferry: cars arrive at random, at the rate one parameter sets, and the ferry leaves on a fixed schedule. Its four model parameters are the number of ferries, the car spaces on each, the time between departures and the arrival rate; the setup gives no values. One trial is one day. The model leaves out weather, and no question turns on it. Shapes, in served order: q1 `threshold-ahead`, q2 `possible-worst`, q3 `past-chance`, q4 `better-ahead`. Between them the four carry all five cases.

Rule on what the learner says the answer would be about, not on the words they use. A frequency in an answer settles nothing by itself: q1's answer is a setting that makes a frequency hold, and q3's is a frequency in a replayed past, and neither is "how often, under that change". An answer that names two kinds without settling on one gets no credit.

### q1

- **goal:** `c-name-question-kind`
- **cases:** target-setting, what-if-ahead, chance-in-it
- **answer:** What setting reaches a target: Dana wants the number of car spaces at which no driver waits more than half an hour on at least 9 days in 10. Phrasings that count: "the setting that gets it there", "how many spaces it would take to hit that", "the size of boat needed to meet that goal", "the smallest number of spaces that keeps waits under half an hour often enough". Lure phrasings that don't count: "how often drivers would wait under a bigger boat" or "what the waits are likely to be with the new boat" (likely outcomes, pulled by the frequency phrase), and "what would have happened with a bigger boat" (a counterfactual past, pulled by "What if").
- **credit:** full when the answer says Dana wants a value for one setting (the car spaces) that makes the wait outcome reach the stated level, by that label or by saying the answer would be a number of spaces; none when it names another kind, or names this kind and another without settling on this one.
- **tutor note:** if they say "how often waits stay under half an hour", ask what Dana would be handed back as the answer: a percentage, or a number of spaces?

### q2

- **goal:** `c-name-question-kind`
- **cases:** can-happen, worst-case-mention
- **answer:** Whether something can happen at all: Marcus wants to know if a car that arrives in the morning can still be waiting at the last sailing, under this model. Phrasings that count: "whether it's possible", "if it can ever occur", "whether the model ever produces that", "a yes or no on whether it could happen". Lure phrasings that don't count: "how bad things can get", "the worst wait a driver could face", "the longest anyone ends up waiting" (how bad, pulled by "the worst case").
- **credit:** full when the answer says Marcus wants a yes or no on whether that outcome can come about under the model, by that label or by saying what the answer would have to be; none when it names another kind, or names this kind and another without settling on this one.
- **tutor note:** if they answer "how bad it can get", ask whether Marcus wants a length of wait, or a yes or no about one particular outcome.

### q3

- **goal:** `c-name-question-kind`
- **cases:** chance-in-it
- **answer:** What would have happened if things had gone differently: Priya wants last August replayed with the bridge open, so with a normal arrival rate. Phrasings that count: "a replay of last August with the bridge open", "what last August would have looked like with normal traffic", "a different past". Lure phrasings that don't count: "how often drivers wait more than an hour", "the chance of a long wait with normal traffic" (likely outcomes, pulled by "how often", with no mention that it is about last August replayed).
- **credit:** full when the answer says Priya is asking about a stretch of the past (last August) replayed with one thing changed, by that label or by saying the answer is what that past would have been; none when it names another kind, or names this kind and another without settling on this one.
- **tutor note:** an answer like "how often drivers would have waited an hour last August if the bridge had been open" names the replayed past and counts, even though it says "how often". If they say only "how often drivers wait more than an hour", ask which days Priya is asking about.

### q4

- **goal:** `c-name-question-kind`
- **cases:** what-if-ahead
- **answer:** Which option is better: Tomas names two changes still to be made, a second ferry or halving the time between departures, and wants to know which cuts waits more. Phrasings that count: "which plan wins", "which of the two options cuts waits more", "a comparison of the two changes to pick one". Lure phrasings that don't count: "what would have happened if they had added a ferry" (a counterfactual past, pulled by "What if"), and "how long waits would be with a second ferry" (likely outcomes at one setting, leaving out the comparison).
- **credit:** full when the answer says Tomas wants the two named options compared to find which comes out ahead, by that label or by saying the answer would be one of the options; none when it names another kind, or names this kind and another without settling on this one.
- **tutor note:** if they describe the outcome of one option only, ask how many options Tomas mentions and what he wants done with them.
