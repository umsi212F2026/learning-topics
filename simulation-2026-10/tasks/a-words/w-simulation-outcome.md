# Simulation outcome

### vs-answer

A shipping company has a simulation model of one of its cargo ships crossing an ocean. Storms come
up at random along the way and slow the ship. One trial is one crossing, and it records how many
days late the ship arrives. The company wants to know how often a crossing ends more than two days
late.

What is the difference between a simulation outcome of this model and the answer to the company's
question? Answer in two or three sentences, in your own words.

### catch-real-week

A toy store had a simulation model of its holiday week, built to see whether it would run out of
the year's most popular toy. Each trial was one simulated week, recording the day the toy sold out,
if it did. Looking back after the real holiday week, the manager says:

"The simulation outcome was that we sold out on Thursday afternoon, and we turned customers away
all day Friday."

What is wrong with that? Answer in two or three sentences, in your own words.

### catch-pick

A bakery bakes the same number of croissants every Saturday morning. It has a simulation model of
a Saturday: customers arrive at random moments through the day, and each one buys a croissant with
some chance. One trial is one Saturday, and it records how many croissants are left at closing. The
bakery wants to know how many go unsold on a typical Saturday.

Which of these statements uses the words "simulation outcome" correctly?

1. Each customer's arrival time is a simulation outcome, since every trial produces one for each customer.
2. The number of croissants left at closing in one trial is a simulation outcome of that trial.
3. One trial's simulation outcome, 14 left over, tells the bakery how many go unsold on a typical Saturday.
4. Zero croissants left at closing is the simulation outcome, since that is what the bakery hopes for.
