# Model parameter

### catch-limits

A city has a simulation model of its snowplows during a snowstorm. It works from the number of
plows, how fast the snow falls, and how long a plow takes to clear a mile of road, and one trial is
one storm, recording how long each road stays unplowed. The model covers only snow, not ice, and
only the city's main roads, not its side streets.

One of the planners says:

"The model's parameters are its limits: it handles snow but not ice, and main roads but not side
streets. Anything outside those parameters, it can't tell us about."

What is wrong with that? Answer in two or three sentences, in your own words.

### vs-outcome

A pizza shop has a simulation model of a Friday night's deliveries. Orders come in at random
through the evening, the shop chooses how many drivers are on shift, and each driver takes one
order at a time. One trial is one Friday night, and it records how many pizzas reach the customer
more than 45 minutes after the order.

In the pizza shop's model, what is the difference between a model parameter and a simulation
outcome? Answer in two or three sentences, in your own words.

### catch-pick

A hotel with 100 rooms accepts more reservations than it has rooms, because some guests never show
up. It has a simulation model of one night: the hotel picks how many reservations to accept, and
each guest who booked shows up with a chance of 9 in 10. One trial is one night, and it records how
many guests arrive to find no room left.

Which of these statements uses the words "model parameter" correctly?

1. The number of guests turned away is a model parameter, since the hotel wants to keep it low.
2. How many reservations to accept is a model parameter, since the hotel fixes it before a run.
3. Whether each guest shows up is a model parameter, since the model settles it guest by guest.
4. The hotel's star rating is a model parameter, since it affects how many guests book.
