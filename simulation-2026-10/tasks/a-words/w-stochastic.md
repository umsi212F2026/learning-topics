# Stochastic

### catch-untrustworthy

A city's bike-share team has a model of one weekday morning at its stations. Each hour, how many
riders take a bike from each station, and where they return it, is drawn at random around that
station's usual numbers. The team wants to know how often a busy downtown station runs out of bikes
before 9 a.m.

One of the team says:

"This model is stochastic, so we can't trust what it tells us. Run it twice and the station runs
out once and not the other time. We should build a deterministic version instead, so we get the
real answer."

What is wrong with that? Answer in two or three sentences, in your own words.

### vs-deterministic

What is the difference between a stochastic model and a deterministic one? Answer in two or three
sentences, in your own words.

### catch-pick

A dining hall has a model of its lunch line. Students join the line at the rate the dining hall
expects for each five-minute stretch: a few at 11:30, a rush at noon, fewer after 12:30. Each
student takes exactly 30 seconds to serve. The model reports the longest the line gets.

Which of these statements uses the word "stochastic" correctly?

1. The model would be stochastic if it replayed the arrival times recorded at a real lunch hour, when students do arrive at random.
2. The model is stochastic, since its line grows during the noon rush and shrinks after it.
3. The model would be stochastic if each student's serving time were drawn at random.
4. The model is stochastic, since each student's wait depends on how long everyone ahead takes.
