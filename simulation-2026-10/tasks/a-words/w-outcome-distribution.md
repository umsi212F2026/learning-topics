# Outcome distribution

### vs-average

A bottling plant has a simulation model of one week on its production line. Machines break down at
random, and one trial records how many hours the line stood still that week. After 5,000 trials,
an engineer reports that the line stands still for 6 hours in an average week.

What is the difference between the outcome distribution of these trials and the average outcome?
Answer in two or three sentences, in your own words.

### catch-one-summer

A mountain hut collects rainwater in a tank for the hikers who stay there. Its keeper has a
simulation model of one summer: rain falls on random days, and each night's hikers use some of the
water. One trial is one summer, and it records how many days the tank was empty. The keeper runs
the model once and says:

"Here is the outcome distribution: the tank was empty for 2 days in June, none in July and 5 in
August."

What is wrong with that? Answer in two or three sentences, in your own words.

### catch-pick

A weather station on a remote island runs on a solar panel and a battery. Its operators have a
simulation model of one winter month there: each day is cloudy with a chance of 1 in 3, and one
trial records how many days the battery ran flat. They have run 2,000 trials with the panel and
battery they have now.

Which of these statements uses the words "outcome distribution" correctly?

1. How many of the 2,000 months had 0 flat days, how many 1, 2 and so on, is the outcome distribution.
2. The average across the 2,000 trials, 1.5 flat days a month, is the outcome distribution.
3. These 2,000 months pooled with 2,000 run with a bigger battery make one outcome distribution.
4. The 1 in 3 chance that the model gives each day of being cloudy is the outcome distribution.
