This made-up model follows a car ferry that crosses a river between two towns, loading cars in the order they reach the dock and leaving on a fixed schedule. It leaves out the weather: every crossing runs on schedule, whatever the wind or fog. Its model parameters are the number of ferries on the route, the number of car spaces on each ferry, the time between departures, and the rate at which cars arrive at the dock. One simulation trial is one day of sailings, from the first to the last, and records how long each car waits at the dock and how many are still waiting when the last ferry leaves. In each minute of the day, a car pulls up to the dock with the chance set by the arrival rate.

Each question below comes from a different person who wants this simulation to answer one question. Each question is about this simulation alone.

### q1

Dana says: "What if the ferry company buys a bigger boat to replace the one it runs now? How many car spaces would the new boat need for no driver to wait more than half an hour, on at least 9 days in 10?"

What kind of question is Dana asking? Say it in a sentence, in your own words.

### q2

Marcus says: "The worst case drivers talk about is pulling up to the dock in the morning and still being in line when the last ferry of the day leaves. Under this model, could that ever occur?"

What kind of question is Marcus asking? Say it in a sentence, in your own words.

### q3

Priya says: "Last August the bridge upstream was closed for repairs, and far more cars than usual came to the ferry. If the bridge had stayed open and the ferry had seen its normal flow of cars, how often would drivers have waited more than an hour?"

What kind of question is Priya asking? Say it in a sentence, in your own words.

### q4

Tomas says: "What if the company puts a second ferry on the route, or keeps one ferry and halves the time between its departures? Which would cut drivers' waits at the dock more?"
