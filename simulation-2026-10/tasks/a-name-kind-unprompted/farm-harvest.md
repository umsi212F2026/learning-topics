This made-up model follows one wheat field on a farm through a growing season: the crop grows a little on each day it has enough water and is set back on each day the soil is too dry. It leaves out pests and plant disease: the only thing that holds the crop back is the water it gets. Its model parameters are the chance of rain on any one day, the amount of water the irrigation system gives the field on each dry day, and the planting date. One simulation trial is one season, from planting to harvest, and records the tons of grain harvested and the number of days the soil was too dry. Each day's weather is drawn at random, with rain falling on that day with the chance set by the first parameter.

Each question below comes from a different person who wants this simulation to answer one question. Each question is about this simulation alone.

### q1

Anika says: "Over fifty seasons at the farm's current settings, in the rarest and driest years, how small does the harvest get?"

What kind of question is Anika asking? Say it in a sentence, in your own words.

### q2

Bjorn says: "What if the farm puts in a drip irrigation system? How much water would it need to give the field on each dry day for the harvest to reach at least 40 tons in 9 seasons out of 10?"

What kind of question is Bjorn asking? Say it in a sentence, in your own words.

### q3

Chidi says: "With rain coming at random from one day to the next, is there any chance of a season, even one, in which the soil never once gets too dry?"

What kind of question is Chidi asking? Say it in a sentence, in your own words.

### q4

Nadia says: "The worst case for the farm is a season so dry that the harvest comes in below half its usual size. Of the two plans the family is weighing, planting two weeks earlier or giving the field more water on each dry day, which makes that rarer?"

What kind of question is Nadia asking? Say it in a sentence, in your own words.
