This made-up simulation models one year of flooding in a river town: storms upstream raise the
river, and when it rises above the town's levee, water spills into the low streets until the
town's pumps clear it. It leaves out the riverbanks wearing away: the channel keeps the same shape
in the model from year to year. Its model parameters are the height of the levee, the number of
storms each spring, and how much water the town's pumps can clear each hour. One trial is one
simulated year, and it records the river's highest level, the number of days water stood in the
low streets, and which houses took water. Chance is built into its rules: storms arrive on random
days, at the rate the second parameter sets, and each storm drops a random amount of rain.

Each question below comes from a different person who wants this simulation to answer one
question. Each question is about this simulation alone.
