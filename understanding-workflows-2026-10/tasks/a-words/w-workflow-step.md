# Workflow step

### vs-subprocess

A conference organizer has drawn her workflow with three steps: "Book the venue", "Send
invitations", and "Arrange catering". "Arrange catering" is drawn with a plus sign, and opens into
a diagram of its own: get quotes, choose a caterer, confirm the menu. "Book the venue" usually takes
her two months; "Arrange catering" takes about two weeks.

In this diagram, what is the difference between a workflow step and a subprocess? Answer in two or
three sentences, in your own words.

### catch-size

Two students are reviewing a diagram of how their club runs its annual conference. One of them
says:

"'Plan the conference' can't be a workflow step. It's far too big a job. A step has to be one small
action, like 'Book the room'."

What is wrong with that? Answer in two or three sentences, in your own words.

### catch-pick

A city library has drawn its workflow for a new library card. It starts from "Application
received". The clerk does "Check proof of address", and then the flow divides at "Address in
city?". On the yes path, "Issue card" is drawn with a plus sign and opens into a diagram of its
own: print the card, register it, hand it over. The run ends at "Card issued".

Which of these statements uses the words "workflow step" correctly?

1. "Application received" is the first workflow step, since every run starts from it.
2. "Address in city?" is a workflow step, since it is where the clerk decides on the address.
3. "Issue card" is one workflow step, even though a diagram of its own sits underneath it.
4. The Clerk lane is one long workflow step, since one person does every action in it.
