# Workflow outcome

### vs-trigger

A small company has two workflows. Its hiring workflow ends either in "Offer accepted" or in "No
hire". Its onboarding workflow, which sets a new employee up with a laptop, an email account and a
first-week schedule, starts from "Offer accepted".

In this situation, what is the difference between a workflow outcome and a trigger, given that
"Offer accepted" appears in both workflows? Answer in two or three sentences, in your own words.

### catch-action

A software team draws its workflow for handling bug reports. A report arrives, a developer tries to
reproduce the bug, and then either fixes it or closes the report as "can't reproduce". One team
member labels the end of the diagram:

"Outcome: developer fixes the bug."

What is wrong with that? Answer in two or three sentences, in your own words.

### catch-pick

A city has a workflow for pothole reports. A resident reports a pothole, and an inspector visits.
If the road belongs to the city, a crew fills the pothole; if it belongs to the county, the report
is passed to the county and closed.

Which of these statements uses the words "workflow outcome" correctly?

1. One workflow outcome is "Pothole filled"; another is "Report passed to the county".
2. The workflow outcome is "Crew fills the pothole", the last thing done in a run.
3. The workflow outcome is "Pothole reported", the one condition every run comes from.
4. The workflow outcome is fewer complaints about the city's roads by the end of the year.
