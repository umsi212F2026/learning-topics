# Handoff

### vs-control-flow

A dental office has drawn its workflow for a checkup, with two lanes, Receptionist and Dentist. In
the Receptionist lane, "Check in the patient" has a solid arrow to "Pull up the chart", which has a
solid arrow across into the Dentist lane to "Examine the patient". That leads by a solid arrow to
"Write treatment notes", which has a dotted line to a file, "Treatment plan". After two more
dentist steps, the last step, in the Receptionist lane, is "Book the follow-up", which has a dotted
line from the "Treatment plan" file.

Name one place in this diagram where there is control flow but no handoff. Give the two steps at
its ends.

### catch-every-arrow

A public library has drawn its workflow for returned books, with two lanes, Desk clerk and Shelver.
In the Desk clerk lane, "Scan the returned book" has a solid arrow to "Check for damage", which has
a solid arrow to "Put it on the cart". From there a solid arrow crosses into the Shelver lane, to
"Reshelve the book". A volunteer, describing the diagram, says:

"There are three handoffs here, one at each arrow, starting with the one from 'Scan the returned
book' to 'Check for damage'."

What is wrong with that? Answer in two or three sentences, in your own words.

### catch-pick

A landscaping company has drawn its workflow for a garden job, with two lanes, Office manager and
Crew lead. In the Office manager lane, "Take the booking" has a dotted line to a "Booking log"
file and a solid arrow to "Write the job sheet", which has a dotted line to a "Job sheet" file. A
solid arrow crosses into the Crew lead lane, to "Load the truck", which has a dotted line from the
"Job sheet" file and is done the next morning. "Load the truck" has a solid arrow to "Drive to the
site", and on to "Finish the job". A solid arrow then crosses back to the Office manager lane, to
"Send the bill", which has a dotted line from the "Booking log" file.

Which of these statements uses the word "handoff" correctly?

1. The job sheet, written by the office manager and read by the crew lead, is a handoff.
2. The arrow from "Take the booking" to "Write the job sheet" is a handoff: it says what runs next.
3. The booking log, which the office manager writes and reads again to bill, is a handoff.
4. The arrow from "Load the truck" to "Drive to the site" is a handoff, as the truck changes place.
