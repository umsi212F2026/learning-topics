# Executor

### vs-instructions

A design firm screens job applications with a one-page guide: "Reject any application with no
portfolio link. Flag every applicant with design experience for an interview." For a month a
recruiter did the screening with the guide. Then the firm handed the same guide, unchanged, to an
AI agent. The agent flagged several applicants whose experience was in interior design; the
recruiter had passed over applicants like them.

In this situation, what is the difference between an executor and the instructions it follows?
Answer in two or three sentences, in your own words.

### catch-same

A support team labels incoming emails by following a skill file, "label-emails.md". For months a
teammate's AI agent carried out the labeling with it and the labels were right. Last week the
team switched to a cheaper AI agent, keeping the skill file exactly as it was, and labels started
coming out wrong. A team member writes:

"Our executor hasn't changed since spring: label-emails.md is word for word the same. So the
labeling problem can't be on the executor's side."

What is wrong with that? Answer in two or three sentences, in your own words.

### catch-pick

A newsroom's workflow has a step "Check the headline against the style guide". The style guide is
a document written by the editor-in-chief. On weekdays the copy editor does the check; on weekends
an AI agent does it, following the same style guide. Weekend headlines have more errors.

Which of these statements uses the word "executor" correctly?

1. The style guide is the executor of the step, since every check made comes from it.
2. The editor-in-chief is the executor of every check, since she wrote the style guide.
3. The step has two executors: the copy editor on weekdays and the agent on weekends.
4. The step has no executor on weekends, since no person is doing the check on those days.
