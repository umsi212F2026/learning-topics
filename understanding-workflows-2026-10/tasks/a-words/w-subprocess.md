# Subprocess

### vs-child-process

An office has a workflow for setting up a new employee's laptop. One step, "Install the printer
driver", is drawn with a plus sign and opens into a diagram of its own: download the driver, run
the installer, print a test page. When the IT assistant runs the installer, the installer starts a
second program in the background that copies files into place.

In this situation, what is the difference between a subprocess and a child process? Answer in two
or three sentences, in your own words.

### catch-pick

A school's workflow for getting a new student's laptop ready includes "Download the installer",
"Install Node" and "Check the install". "Install Node" is drawn with a plus sign. While it runs,
the Node installer starts several helper programs of its own.

Which of these statements uses the word "subprocess" correctly?

1. "Install Node" is a subprocess, since its plus sign means a diagram of its own sits underneath.
2. "Install Node" is a subprocess, since the installer starts other programs while it is running.
3. "Download the installer" is a subprocess, since on a slow connection it takes the longest.
4. "Check the install" is a subprocess, since it runs in the background while the student works.

### catch-many-jobs

A hospital has drawn its workflow for discharging a patient. One step, "Arrange home care", is a
plain rectangle with no plus sign. In practice it takes a social worker several days: calling
agencies, checking insurance, and booking a nurse's first visit. A nurse looking at the diagram
says:

"'Arrange home care' is a subprocess, since it's made of lots of smaller jobs."

What is wrong with that? Answer in two or three sentences, in your own words.
