# Rubric: Workflow step

If the learner misses a question here, set a DEFINE or INTERPRET move for workflow step live, as
help (it is recorded as helped and doesn't count), then come back to a production question.

A workflow step is one action, at whatever grain the diagram chose to show: "Plan the conference"
and "Book the room" can each be one step, in diagrams drawn at different levels. A subprocess is a
step with a diagram of its own underneath it, drawn with a plus sign. What makes one is that
diagram, not the size of the job. "Action" and "task" are other names for a step, not confusables.

### vs-subprocess

- **goal:** `w-workflow-step`
- **move:** DISTINGUISH
- **answer:** A step is one action shown at whatever grain this diagram chose. A subprocess is a
  step that has its own diagram underneath: "Arrange catering", with its plus sign, opens into
  quotes, choosing and confirming. Size is not what decides it: "Book the venue" takes longer but
  has no diagram underneath, so it is a plain step.
- **credit:** full for naming that a subprocess has its own diagram underneath it and a plain step
  does not; saying a subprocess is still a step is welcome but not required. Half for the right
  difference with "Book the venue" called a subprocess because it takes longer. None for size or
  importance alone, such as "a subprocess is a bigger step".
- **tutor note:** a learner who says "a subprocess is a bigger step" is reaching for size; ask
  which of the organizer's three steps is the longest, and which one has the plus sign.

### catch-size

- **goal:** `w-workflow-step`
- **move:** CATCH
- **answer:** A step is one action at whatever grain the diagram chose to show, not a job of some
  fixed small size. In a diagram drawn at the level of the club's whole year, "Plan the
  conference" is one step; in a finer one, "Book the room" is.
- **credit:** full for naming that a step's size is set by the grain the diagram chose, so a big
  job can be one step. Half for saying it could be a subprocess (which is still a step) with
  nothing on grain. None for a different quibble alone, such as that "Book the room" is not a
  small job either.

### catch-pick

- **goal:** `w-workflow-step`
- **move:** CATCH
- **type:** mcq
- **answer:** 3
- **credit:** full for 3 only. A subprocess is still a step, one action shown at this diagram's
  grain, with its detail drawn underneath. Choice 1 is the trigger, a condition, not an action.
  Choice 2 is a gateway, where the flow divides; the deciding was done in the step before it.
  Choice 4 is a lane, which says whose job a step is.
