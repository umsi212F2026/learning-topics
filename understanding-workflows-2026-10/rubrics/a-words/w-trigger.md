# Rubric: Trigger

If the learner misses a question here, set a DEFINE or INTERPRET move for trigger live, as help (it
is recorded as helped and doesn't count), then come back to a production question.

A trigger is what starts a run: a person's action (a form sent in, a button pressed), a time
arriving (the last business day of the month) or a message (an email landing). It is not a step
done in the run, not even the first one; a step is done once the run has started. A trigger can be
something a person does, so "it is an action" never rules a trigger out. "Start event" is another
name for it, not a confusable.

### vs-step

- **goal:** `w-trigger`
- **move:** DISTINGUISH
- **answer:** The trigger is the student sending in the reset form ("Reset request received"):
  that is what starts a run. A step is something done in the run once it has started, such as the
  technician verifying the caller's identity. The student's action starts the run, the
  technician's is part of it; even the first step is a step, not the trigger.
- **credit:** full for naming that the trigger is what starts a run and a step is something done
  in the run; examples are not required, but any given must be matched correctly, and calling
  "Verify the caller's identity" the trigger makes it a miss. Half for "the trigger comes first
  and steps come after" with nothing on starting the run versus being done in it. Half at most
  for resting the difference on "nobody does a trigger" or "a trigger is not an action", since
  here the trigger is the student's own action. None for an incidental difference alone, such as
  their shapes on the diagram.
- **tutor note:** a learner who says a trigger can't be something a person does is holding the
  word too narrowly; ask who sent in the reset form, and whether the run had started before they
  did.

### catch-pick

- **goal:** `w-trigger`
- **move:** CATCH
- **type:** mcq
- **answer:** 2
- **credit:** full for 2 only. The patron sending in the form is a person's action, and it is what
  starts a run. Choice 1 is the first step, done in the run once it has started. Choice 3 is the
  outcome, where a run ends. Choice 4 is an actor who carries out steps, not anything that starts
  a run.
- **tutor note:** a learner who picks 1 is reading the trigger as the first thing done in the
  run; ask what had to happen before the librarian had anything to search for. A learner who
  rejects 2 because the patron is doing something is holding the word too narrowly; a person's
  action can be a trigger.

### catch-payroll

- **goal:** `w-trigger`
- **move:** CATCH
- **answer:** Exporting the timesheets is the first step, done inside the run. The trigger is
  what starts the run: the last business day of the month arriving. The run has already started
  by the time the clerk exports anything.
- **credit:** full for naming that exporting is a step done in the run, not what starts it;
  naming the last business day as the trigger is welcome but not required. Half for "the trigger
  is the date" with nothing on why exporting isn't one. Half at most for saying exporting can't
  be the trigger because a person does it, since a person's action can be a trigger; the error
  is that this one happens after the run has started. None for a different quibble alone, such
  as that the accountant should export the timesheets.
