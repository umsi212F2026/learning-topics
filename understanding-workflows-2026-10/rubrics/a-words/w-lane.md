# Rubric: Lane

If the learner misses a question here, set a DEFINE or INTERPRET move for lane live, as help (it is
recorded as helped and doesn't count), then come back to a production question.

A lane is the band across a diagram that says whose job a step is: a step sits in the lane of the
actor who does it. It names who, not the job, not a stage, and not a kind of work. A step drawn
across two lanes is one step both actors share. "Swimlane" is another name for a lane. The word
has no confusable in goals.md, so its bank is CATCH questions only.

### catch-phase

- **goal:** `w-lane`
- **move:** CATCH
- **answer:** A lane says who does a step, not what the step is about. The server takes the order,
  so "Take the order" belongs in the Server lane, however much the order is about food.
- **credit:** full for naming that a lane says whose job a step is, so the step goes in the lane
  of whoever does it, here the server. Half for "it belongs in the Server lane" with no reason
  that rests on who does it. None for a different quibble alone, such as that the restaurant needs
  a fourth lane for the host.

### catch-pick

- **goal:** `w-lane`
- **move:** CATCH
- **type:** mcq
- **answer:** 2
- **credit:** full for 2 only. A step's lane says who does it. Choice 1 reads the lane as the job
  rather than whose job; the lane holds only this workflow's steps. Choice 3 reads lanes as stages
  in time, but order runs along the arrows, not across the lanes. Choice 4 misreads a step that
  spans two lanes: it is one step the treasurer and the bank share, done once.
