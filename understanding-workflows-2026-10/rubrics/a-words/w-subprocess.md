# Rubric: Subprocess

If the learner misses a question here, set a DEFINE or INTERPRET move for subprocess live, as help
(it is recorded as helped and doesn't count), then come back to a production question.

A subprocess is a step with a diagram of its own underneath it, drawn collapsed with a plus sign.
It belongs to how a workflow is drawn. A child process is something else: a running program that
another running program has started on a computer. "Sub-workflow" is another name for a
subprocess, not a confusable.

### vs-child-process

- **goal:** `w-subprocess`
- **move:** DISTINGUISH
- **answer:** "Install the printer driver" is a subprocess: a step in the diagram with a diagram of
  its own underneath. The background copier is a child process: a program the installer started
  on the laptop. One is about how the workflow is drawn, the other about programs running on a
  computer.
- **credit:** full for naming that a subprocess is a step with its own diagram and a child process
  is a running program started by another program; matching them to the scenario is not required,
  but any match given must be right. Half for "a subprocess is part of the workflow and a child
  process is part of a program" with nothing on the subprocess having its own diagram. None for
  an incidental difference alone, such as that the child process runs in the background.

### catch-pick

- **goal:** `w-subprocess`
- **move:** CATCH
- **type:** mcq
- **answer:** 1
- **credit:** full for 1 only. A subprocess is a step with its own diagram underneath, which the
  plus sign marks. Choice 2 reads the word as a child process: programs the installer starts are
  something else. Choice 3 reads it as a long step, but length does not make one. Choice 4 reads
  "sub" as running in the background, which is not what the word names.
- **tutor note:** a learner who picks 2 has a programmer's meaning in mind, which is a real one;
  ask what the plus sign on the diagram tells them, and whether the diagram would change if the
  installer started no helpers.

### catch-many-jobs

- **goal:** `w-subprocess`
- **move:** CATCH
- **answer:** A subprocess is a step with a diagram of its own underneath it, marked with a plus
  sign. "Arrange home care" has none in this diagram, so here it is a plain step, however many
  smaller jobs the social worker does inside it. It would become a subprocess only if someone drew
  those jobs as a diagram beneath it.
- **credit:** full for naming that what makes a subprocess is its own diagram underneath, which
  this step doesn't have. Half for "there's no plus sign" with nothing on what the plus sign
  marks. None for a different quibble alone, such as that the step should be split into several
  steps.
