# Rubric: Executor

If the learner misses a question here, set a DEFINE or INTERPRET move for executor live, as help
(it is recorded as helped and doesn't count), then come back to a production question.

An executor is whatever carries out a step: a person, an AI agent, a script. The instructions it
follows (a guide, a skill file, a script's code) are something else, and the two can be swapped
independently: the same instructions in the hands of a different executor can give different
results. "Actor" is another name for an executor, not a confusable. Everyday shorthand that gives
the instructions credit ("the guide decides who gets an interview") is fine and is not a misuse;
the misuse is calling the instructions the executor, or treating the executor as unchanged
because the instructions are.

### vs-instructions

- **goal:** `w-executor`
- **move:** DISTINGUISH
- **answer:** The guide is the instructions, and it stayed the same throughout. The executor is
  whatever carried out the screening: first the recruiter, then the AI agent. Swapping the
  executor while keeping the instructions changed the results, since the two read "design
  experience" differently.
- **credit:** full for naming that the executor is what carries out the step and the instructions
  are what it follows, so one can change while the other stays the same; the different results
  as evidence of that are welcome but not required. Half for the right difference with only the
  recruiter counted as an executor and the agent left out or called part of the instructions.
  None for an incidental difference alone, such as that the guide is written down.

### catch-same

- **goal:** `w-executor`
- **move:** CATCH
- **answer:** label-emails.md is the instructions, not the executor. The executor is whatever
  carries out the labeling, and it did change last week, from the teammate's agent to the cheaper
  one. Same instructions with a new executor is exactly where to look for the problem.
- **credit:** full for naming that the skill file is the instructions and the executor is the
  agent carrying them out, which did change. Half for "the agent changed" with nothing on the
  file being the instructions rather than the executor. None for a different quibble alone, such
  as that the team should not have switched to a cheaper agent.

### catch-pick

- **goal:** `w-executor`
- **move:** CATCH
- **type:** mcq
- **answer:** 3
- **credit:** full for 3 only. Whatever carries out the step is its executor, and here that
  changes with the day while the instructions stay the same. Choice 1 calls the instructions the
  executor. Choice 2 calls their author the executor, but she carries out none of the checks.
  Choice 4 reads executor as a person only; the agent carries out the weekend checks.
- **tutor note:** a learner who picks 1 may be extending everyday shorthand ("the style guide
  decides"), which is fine as shorthand; ask who or what would have to change for the weekend
  errors to stop if the guide stayed as it is.
