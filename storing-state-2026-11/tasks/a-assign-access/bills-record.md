Rosa keeps her household's bills in a bills record, which she starts fresh each January and which
holds about four hundred entries by December. Every hour from 8 a.m. to 10 p.m., a program on her
laptop saves any new statements that arrive by email from the household's utility companies, phone
company and bank into a folder. Then the bill agent, following a skill file Rosa wrote, reads each
new statement, decides which household category it belongs to, and adds an entry to the bills
record with the company, the amount, the due date and the category. A run takes about fifteen
minutes. A few bills still come on paper, and most evenings, often while a run is going, Rosa adds
an entry by hand for each paper bill that came that day. She wants every entry left exactly as it
was entered, so the record shows what each company billed if she ever has to dispute a charge. On
the last day of each month, the budget program goes over the whole bills record and adds up what
the household has spent in each category so far this year; it never changes or adds anything in
the bills record.
