# Rubric: Query

If the learner misses a question here, set a DEFINE or INTERPRET move for query live, as help (it
is recorded as helped and doesn't count), then come back to a production question.

A query asks a store for just the records that match, rather than reading through all of it. The
reading's example is asking the grades sheet which submissions have a drafted score but no final
score yet. What comes back is only the matching records; reading every record and picking out the
ones wanted is reading it through, even when the result is the same. Adding or changing records is
not a query either. A search is the course's contrast: looking through text for the places a word
or phrase appears, as in jumping to every mention of a word in a notes file. A search finds
wording wherever it turns up; a query asks for the records that meet a condition, from a store
whose records have parts, like a date or a billed flag, to match on.

### vs-search

- **goal:** `w-query`
- **move:** DISTINGUISH
- **type:** compound
- **tutor note:** the billing program makes a query: it asks the store for the records that meet a
  condition (this week, not billed) and gets back only those. Rosa does a search: she looks through
  free-form text for every place a word appears. A learner who calls the billing program a search
  may be reading the two words as the same thing; ask what the program asked for and what came back.
  One who calls Rosa's step a query may think any looking-up is a query; ask what records the notes
  file has for a condition to match.

### catch-pick

- **goal:** `w-query`
- **move:** CATCH
- **type:** mcq
- **answer:** 4
- **credit:** full for 4 only. Asking the record for just the rows that match (last year, eggs) is a
  query. Choice 1 gets what comes back wrong: a query returns only the matching rows, not all of
  them. Choice 2 stretches the term to any access: adding sales is a write, not asking for records.
  Choice 3 calls reading through a query; reading every row and picking out the eggs is reading it
  through, even though it finds the same rows.

### catch-diary

- **goal:** `w-query`
- **move:** CATCH
- **answer:** She didn't run a query; she read the whole diary through. A query asks a store for
  just the entries that match, here the March ones, rather than going through all of it. Scrolling
  from the top and picking out March herself is reading it through, which is fine for 200 entries.
- **credit:** full for naming that she read through every entry herself, while a query asks the
  store for just the matching ones, so this was not a query. Saying that reading through is fine at
  this size is welcome but not required. Half for "she just scrolled" with nothing on what a query
  would have done instead. None for a different quibble alone, such as that she should use a
  spreadsheet, or that she should have searched for the word "March".
- **tutor note:** a learner who says it counts because she got only the March total is judging by
  the result; ask how many entries she had to look at to get it.
