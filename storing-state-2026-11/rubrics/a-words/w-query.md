# Rubric: Query

If the learner misses a question here, set a DEFINE or INTERPRET move for query live, as help (it
is recorded as helped and doesn't count), then come back to a production question.

A query asks a store for just the records that match, rather than going over all of it. The
reading's example is asking the grades sheet which submissions have a drafted score but no final
score yet. What comes back is only the matching records. Adding or changing records is not a query.
A full scan is the course's contrast: going over the whole thing, start to end. Which one fits
turns on what is needed: particular records call for a query, and every record calls for a full
scan. Going over every record and picking out the ones wanted is a full scan, not a query, even
when the result is the same; with a few hundred records that is fine, and with tens of thousands
a query is the better choice.

### vs-full-scan

- **goal:** `w-query`
- **move:** DISTINGUISH
- **type:** compound
- **tutor note:** the copying program needs every walk in the store, so going over the whole thing
  is right: a full scan. The billing program needs only the week's unbilled walks, a few dozen out
  of about 30,000, so asking for just those is a query; at this size, going over every walk each
  Friday is what the reading says to avoid. A learner who calls the copying program a query may
  think any program reading a store is querying it; ask which walks it can leave out. One who calls
  the billing program a full scan may be thinking of how they would find the walks by hand; ask
  how many of the 30,000 walks the billing program actually needs.

### catch-pick

- **goal:** `w-query`
- **move:** CATCH
- **type:** mcq
- **answer:** 4
- **credit:** full for 4 only. Asking the record for just the rows that match (last year, eggs) is a
  query. Choice 1 gets what comes back wrong: a query returns only the matching rows, not all of
  them. Choice 2 stretches the term to any access: adding sales is a write, not asking for records.
  Choice 3 calls a full scan a query; going over every row and picking out the eggs is a full
  scan, even though it finds the same rows.

### catch-diary

- **goal:** `w-query`
- **move:** CATCH
- **answer:** She didn't run a query; she did a full scan of the diary. A query asks a store for
  just the entries that match, here the March ones, rather than going over all of it. Scrolling
  from the top and picking out March herself is a full scan, which is fine for 200 entries.
- **credit:** full for naming that she went over every entry herself, while a query asks the store
  for just the matching ones, so this was not a query. Saying that a full scan is fine at this size
  is welcome but not required. Half for "she just scrolled" with nothing on what a query would have
  done instead. None for a different quibble alone, such as that she should use a spreadsheet.
- **tutor note:** a learner who says it counts because she got only the March total is judging by
  the result; ask how many entries she had to look at to get it.
