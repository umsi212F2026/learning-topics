# Rubric: Single source of truth

If the learner misses a question here, set a DEFINE or INTERPRET move for single source of truth
live, as help (it is recorded as helped and doesn't count), then come back to a production
question.

A single source of truth is the one place a piece of information is kept and changed, which every
copy or view of it is taken from, so there is never a question of which version to believe. Copies
taken from it, such as a printed list, don't break it; a second place where the same thing is typed
in and changed separately does. A backup is the course's contrast: a copy saved so the information
can be recovered if the original is lost. It is taken from the source of truth, is never where
changes are made, and is out of date as soon as the source changes. Having a single source of truth
does nothing to protect against losing it, which is what a backup is for.

### vs-backup

- **goal:** `w-single-source-truth`
- **move:** DISTINGUISH
- **type:** compound
- **tutor note:** the external drive copy is a backup: saved on a schedule, opened only to recover
  a lost spreadsheet, and never where changes are made. The registrar's spreadsheet is the single
  source of truth: it is where phone numbers are changed, and the coaches' lists are taken from it.
  A learner who calls the external copy a single source of truth may be reading the term as "the
  safe copy"; ask where a new phone number gets written. One who calls the spreadsheet a backup may
  think any saved file is a backup; ask what the spreadsheet would be a copy of.

### catch-pick

- **goal:** `w-single-source-truth`
- **move:** CATCH
- **type:** mcq
- **answer:** 2
- **credit:** full for 2 only. The coordinator's spreadsheet is the one place the shifts are kept
  and changed, and the door sheet is printed from it. Choice 1 stretches the term to the copy people
  read most; being read is not being the place changes are made. Choice 3 takes "single" to mean no
  copies may exist; a copy taken from the source, as the door sheet is, leaves the source single.
  Choice 4 reads the term as its confusable: knowing which version to believe does nothing if the
  spreadsheet is lost, and that is what the nightly copy, a backup, is for.

### catch-copy

- **goal:** `w-single-source-truth`
- **move:** CATCH
- **answer:** The copy on the external drive is a backup, not the single source of truth. The
  spreadsheet is the one place session times are kept and changed, so it is the source of truth.
  The Sunday copy was taken before Wednesday's change, so it still shows Tuesday, which is wrong.
- **credit:** full for naming that the spreadsheet, where times are changed, is the single source
  of truth, and the drive copy is a backup taken from it, so it can be out of date. Saying the copy
  would still show Tuesday is welcome but not required. Half for "the copy is old" or "it's just a
  backup" with nothing on why that means it isn't the source of truth. None for a different quibble
  alone, such as that the center should back up more often.
- **tutor note:** a learner who says the drive copy is safer, so it should be trusted, is reading
  the term as "the safe copy"; ask where the front desk made Wednesday's change.
