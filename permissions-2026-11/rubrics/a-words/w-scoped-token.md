# Rubric: Scoped token

If the learner misses a question here, set a DEFINE or INTERPRET move for scoped token live, as help (it is recorded as helped and doesn't count), then come back to a production question.

A scoped token is a secret that lets its holder do only certain things with an account, not everything the account can do. A password is the course's contrast: it is a secret too, but it signs its holder in as the account's owner, so it lets them do everything the account can. The difference that matters is how much the secret lets its holder do, which is also how much a thief who copies it can do. "Token" here is not the unit of text a model reads and writes; the reading says so in the note after the scoped token bullet. A scoped token also says nothing about files or the network: it limits what can be done with one account, not what an executor can reach on the laptop it runs on.

### vs-password

- **goal:** `w-scoped-token`

- **type:** mcq
- **answer:** 2
- **credit:** full for 2 only.
- **tutor note:** the password lets whoever holds it do anything Hiro's account can, changing prices included; the scoped token lets its holder read orders and nothing else. A learner who picks "Either of them" is reading a scoped token as a password by another name; ask what the token lets its holder do. One who picks "Neither" may think a copied password is useless to someone else; ask what a password lets its holder do.
