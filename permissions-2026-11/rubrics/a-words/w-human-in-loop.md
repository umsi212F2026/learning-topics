# Rubric: Human in the loop

If the learner misses a question here, set a DEFINE or INTERPRET move for human in the loop live, as help (it is recorded as helped and doesn't count), then come back to a production question.

A human in the loop is a person checking or approving what an automated step produced before it takes effect. A person trigger is the course's contrast, from the triggers topic: a person starting a run, which checks nothing the run produces. The difference that matters is what the person does with the step's output: a human in the loop looks at it and can stop it before it takes effect; a person who starts the run never sees it. Timing matters too: reading the output after it has taken effect can't stop a bad result. A human in the loop also leaves the step's reach alone; it shrinks the blast radius by catching a bad result, not by narrowing what the executor can touch. The reading defines the term under "Checking what a step produced before it takes effect", with the TA reviewing every draft before grades are posted.

- **goal:** `w-human-in-loop`

### catch-after-sending

- **goal:** `w-human-in-loop`
- **move:** CATCH
- **answer:** A human in the loop checks what a step produced before it takes effect. Gideon reads each issue only after it has been sent, so anything wrong in it has already reached the subscribers and his reading can't stop it.
- **credit:** full for naming that a human in the loop checks the output before it takes effect, and Gideon reads it only after it has gone out, so he can't stop a bad issue. Half for "he reads it too late" with nothing on what a human in the loop is for, or for "a human in the loop approves output" with nothing on why Gideon's reading doesn't count. None for a different quibble alone, such as that he should read it more often.
- **tutor note:** a learner who agrees, since Gideon reads carefully, is reading the term as any person looking at the output; ask what Gideon could do about a wrong issue by Friday.
