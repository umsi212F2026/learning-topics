I coordinate the volunteers at our neighborhood makerspace, and members ask to use the 3D
printers through a request form on the makerspace's website. About thirty requests come in a
week, one or two at a time and never many together, and most arrive late in the evening, after
members have finished their designs at home. The website keeps every request with the time it
came in, and it can list all the requests that arrived since any time it is given. An AI agent
reads each request, checks that the design will fit on one of our printers, and emails the member
a time slot. The agent runs on the laptop at the front desk, the makerspace's only computer, which
the first volunteer turns on at 9 every morning and the last one turns off at 9 at night, when we
close. It handles a request in about a minute, and its provider takes up to twenty requests a
minute. The agent emails each reply straight out and keeps no copy, so nothing on the laptop shows
which requests have been answered. Members expect a time slot by the end of the day after they
ask, and one who never hears back usually gives up on the print. Our three printers are named
after the planets nearest the sun.
