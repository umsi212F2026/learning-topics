Shape `unavailable`, variant `machine-off`, case `unavailable`, Medium. The laptop's on-time
differs by hours on two set days a week, so a fixed morning time plainly misses.

Accepted: the coach starting the run by hand when she turns the laptop on; or the laptop being
turned on starting the run (the agent set to run when the laptop starts up), which is an event by
the design rule in Credit and works. Both run once the laptop is on, every day, and take in every
gift up to that morning.

The deciding facts, and the sentences carrying them:

- When the laptop is turned off: "I turn the laptop off, not just close it, at 10:30 every night."
- That its morning on-time varies: "Most mornings I turn it on at 6:30, but on Tuesdays and
  Thursdays, when I drive my daughter to early swim practice, I don't turn it on until about 10,
  and I want the post up as soon as the laptop is on."
- That the post must include that morning's gifts, and gifts come at all hours: "Sponsors give
  through the team's donation page at all hours, including late at night and early in the morning
  while my laptop is off." and "The team wants that morning's gifts in the post too."

Why the other choices fit worse:

- A schedule early enough for most mornings (6:45 a.m.): on Tuesdays and Thursdays it fires while
  the laptop is still off, so no post is written those days.
- A schedule late enough to be safe (10:15 a.m.): on most mornings the post waits more than three
  hours after the laptop is on, when the coach wants it as soon as the laptop is on.
- An event per gift: each gift would start a run that makes a post naming only the gifts since
  the previous post, one sponsor at a time, rather than one post each morning; and gifts that come
  in overnight arrive while the laptop is off.

Never accepted, by name: an evening schedule just before shutdown (say 10:15 p.m.), whose post
lacks that morning's gifts; an event that starts a run for each gift, which would make a post of
only the gifts since the previous post; any fixed morning schedule, early or late. No cost fact is
used against the per-gift event.

An answer that relies on a missed 6:45 run being caught up when the laptop comes back (as the
reading says launchd does after sleep) rests on a fact the scenario contradicts: the laptop is
turned off, not asleep, so that reason is not tied. The scenario says nothing about how reliably
the coach remembers, either way; an answer that says she might forget brings in a fact from
outside the scenario and is not ruled on it.

Irrelevant detail: "The team's robot this year is named Sprocket."

Generic reasons that fail: "a schedule is automatic", "doing it by hand means it gets checked",
"a person can make sure it's done right".

True facts with no bearing on the choice, which also fail: the post goes on the team's page; it
names every sponsor who has given since the last post.

Examples of each kind of tied reason, for the hand start (or the laptop's start-up):

- What makes it good here: "the laptop comes on at 6:30 most days and about 10 on Tuesdays and
  Thursdays, and starting it when she turns the laptop on works for both."
- What would go wrong here without it: "with a fixed 6:45 run, Tuesday's and Thursday's posts
  would never be written, because the laptop is still off."
- What would make a specific other kind worse: "a schedule at 10:30 p.m. would miss the gifts
  that come in overnight and that morning, which the team wants in the post."

### q1

- **goal:** `c-choose-trigger`
- **cases:** unavailable
- **answer:** The coach starting it by hand when she turns the laptop on (or the laptop's start-up
  starting it). The laptop is off from 10:30 at night until 6:30 most mornings and about 10 on
  Tuesdays and Thursdays, so a 6:45 run wouldn't happen on those days, a later fixed time would
  leave the post waiting on most days, and an evening run would miss that morning's gifts.
- **credit:** full when the answer chooses the hand start when the laptop is turned on, or the
  laptop's start-up starting the run, and gives a reason tied to this workflow of any of the three
  kinds: what makes it good here, what would go wrong here without it, or what would make a
  specific other kind worse. The ruling is on whether the design would actually run while the
  laptop is on and produce a post with that morning's gifts, not on whether the reason uses the
  word "available". One tied reason is enough, and a reason that also names what the choice costs
  ("she has to start it each morning, but the laptop is often off at 6:45") still earns full
  credit. A reason resting on a catch-up after sleep, on the irrelevant detail, or on a fact with
  no bearing on the choice is not tied. An answer listing two choices is ruled as choosing neither;
  a combined design is ruled by its main choice. Half when the answer chooses a fixed schedule, an
  evening schedule or a per-gift event with a reason resting on a fact that bears on the choice
  (for example, "a schedule at 6:45, because most mornings the laptop is on at 6:30"), or chooses
  the hand start with a generic, untied or missing reason. None when the choice is not accepted
  and the reason is generic, untied or missing, or no trigger is chosen.
- **tutor note:** the likely miss is a 6:45 a.m. schedule, read off the usual on-time; that is
  half. After the ruling, ask what happens to that run on a Thursday.
