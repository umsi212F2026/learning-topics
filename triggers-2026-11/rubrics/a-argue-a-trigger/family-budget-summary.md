Shape `person-or-schedule`, variant `schedule`, case `not-event`, Hard. A second kind is nearly
as good: the husband is at the family computer by 6, an hour before the summary is due and after
the last orders are in, so his starting it would work too. The key accepts both.

Accepted, each with its defining facts:

- A schedule early on the first of the month (any time after midnight that leaves the summary in
  both inboxes by 7 a.m., such as 1 a.m. or 5 a.m.). Defining facts: the set time, "The summary has
  to be in both our inboxes by 7 a.m. on the first of the month, because we go over it together at
  breakfast."; the steady flow up to midnight, "We log purchases a few times a day all month." and
  "The orders we place online late at night keep coming in right up to midnight on the month's last
  day."; and the one summary of the period, "turns each month's purchases into one summary covering
  the whole month".
- The husband starting it by hand when he sits down at the family computer on the first. Defining
  fact: "My husband is up by 6 every morning, and the first thing he does is sit down at the family
  computer with his coffee.", which puts him at the computer after the last orders are in and an
  hour before the summary is due.

Why the other choices fit worse:

- An event per purchase: each purchase would start a run, dozens a month, where one summary
  covering the whole month is the result, so all but the last run would make a summary nobody
  uses.
- A person starting it the evening before, on the month's last day: orders keep coming in right up
  to midnight, so the summary would miss them.
- A schedule after 7 a.m., or a person starting it after breakfast: the summary is due at 7.

Never accepted, by name: an event that starts a run for each purchase, which would make a summary
of only the purchases so far each time; a person or schedule that starts it before midnight on the
last day, which misses the last orders; anything that starts it after 7 a.m. on the first.

The scenario does not say when the family computer is on, nor how reliably the husband remembers.
An answer for the schedule that says he might forget on the first, once a month, refers to this
workflow's person and timing, and is a tied reason for the schedule; an answer for the person that
assumes the computer is off overnight brings in a fact from outside the scenario.

Irrelevant detail: "We started keeping track after we bought the house."

Generic reasons that fail: "a schedule is automatic", "events are faster", "a person can make sure
it's done right".

True facts with no bearing on the choice, which also fail: the summary splits spending into
groceries, bills, the car and everything else; the computer is in the den.

Examples of each kind of tied reason:

- For the schedule. Good here: "it's one summary of the whole month, due by 7 a.m. on the first,
  so one run early on the first, after the midnight orders, does it." Would go wrong without it:
  "if someone started it before bed on the last day, the late-night orders up to midnight would be
  left out." Makes a specific other kind worse: "running it on each purchase would make dozens of
  summaries a month when only one covering the whole month is wanted."
- For the husband starting it. Good here: "he is at the family computer by 6 every morning, an
  hour before the summary is due and after the last orders are in." Would go wrong without it:
  "started the night before, it would miss the orders that come in up to midnight." Makes a
  specific other kind worse: "an event on each purchase would make a summary dozens of times a
  month, when they want one covering the whole month."

### q1

- **goal:** `c-choose-trigger`
- **cases:** not-event
- **answer:** A schedule early on the first of the month, after midnight and before 7 a.m., since
  it is one summary of the whole month, orders keep coming in right up to midnight, and the summary
  is due by 7. Or the husband starting it when he sits down at the family computer at 6, after the
  last orders are in and an hour before it is due. Either way, not an event per purchase, which
  would make dozens of summaries for one wanted.
- **credit:** full when the answer chooses a schedule that runs after midnight and in time for 7
  a.m. on the first, or the husband starting it when he sits down at 6, and gives a reason tied to
  this workflow of any of the three kinds: what makes that trigger good here, what would go wrong
  here without it, or what would make a specific other kind worse. One tied reason is enough, and
  a reason that also names what the choice costs still earns full credit. A reason resting on the
  irrelevant detail, on a fact with no bearing on the choice, or on one the facts contradict is not
  tied. An answer listing both accepted choices without committing to one is accepted, since the
  key accepts both, and is ruled on its reason; a combined design is ruled by its main choice. Half
  when the answer chooses an event per purchase, or a run before midnight on the last day, with a
  reason resting on a fact that bears on the choice (for example, "each purchase should start a
  run, because purchases come in all month"), or chooses an accepted design with a generic, untied
  or missing reason. None when the choice is not accepted and the reason is generic, untied or
  missing, or no trigger is chosen.
- **tutor note:** the likely miss is a run the evening of the month's last day, by schedule or by
  the husband before bed, which leaves out the orders that come in up to midnight; that is half.
  After the ruling, ask which purchases that summary would leave out.
