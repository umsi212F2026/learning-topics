Shape `unavailable`, variant `person-away`, case `unavailable`, Hard. The coordinator is back by
8:30 almost every week, so a fixed time just after that looks safe; only the late Monday, about
once a month, shows that it would miss.

Accepted: the coordinator starting the run herself once she has typed in the counts. A design
where saving the counts starts the run is ruled as the person starting it, and accepted; so is a
design that checks every few minutes for that week's counts and starts the run once they are in.

The deciding facts, and the sentences carrying them:

- That a run can't begin until she has done the first step: "It can't start on a list until I have
  counted the shelves and typed the counts in, since the list is built from them."
- When she is away: "I am away visiting my sister every weekend."
- That her return, and so the counts, comes at a time that varies, and that she wants the list at
  once: "I count the shelves and type in the counts once I am back on Monday morning, almost always
  between 8 and 8:30, though about once a month traffic holds me up and it is closer to 9:30, and I
  want the list the moment I have typed the counts in, so I can head straight to the wholesale
  market."

Why the other choices fit worse:

- A schedule early enough to catch most Mondays (8:45 a.m., or 8 a.m.): about once a month it
  fires before the counts are in, so the agent has nothing for this week to build the list from.
- A schedule late enough to be safe (9:45 a.m. or later): most weeks the counts are in by 8:30, so
  the list waits more than an hour after she wants it.
- An event other than her own input: nothing in the scenario happens on its own that would mean
  the counts are ready, except her typing them in, which is the person starting it.

Never accepted, by name: a fixed schedule at 8 or 8:45 on Monday, which fires before she is back
on a late Monday; a fixed schedule at 9:45 or later, which leaves the list waiting after an early
return; any run set for the weekend, when she is away and the counts can't have been typed in.

The scenario says nothing about how reliably she remembers, what a run costs or what changes at
the pantry while she is away. An answer that rests on one of those brings in a fact from outside
the scenario. An answer that the hand start is right because she wants the list the moment she has
typed the counts in rests on a fact in the scenario and is accepted; see the activity's
doesn't-show.

Irrelevant detail: "The pantry is in the basement of the old library."

Generic reasons that fail: "a schedule is automatic", "a person can make sure it's done right",
"it's simpler to do it by hand".

True facts with no bearing on the choice, which also fail: the list says what to buy and how much;
she visits her sister.

Examples of each kind of tied reason, for the person starting it:

- What makes it good here: "the run can only start once she has typed in the counts, and that
  happens at a different time on late Mondays, so she should start it when they are in."
- What would go wrong here without it: "a run at a fixed 8:45 would come before the counts on the
  Mondays traffic holds her up, so there'd be nothing to build that week's list from."
- What would make a specific other kind worse: "a schedule at 9:45 would always be after the
  counts, but most weeks she'd wait over an hour for a list she wants the moment they're in."

### q1

- **goal:** `c-choose-trigger`
- **cases:** unavailable
- **answer:** The coordinator should start it herself once she has typed in the counts (or saving
  the counts should start it). The list can't be built until the counts are in, and she types them
  in on her return, usually by 8:30 but about once a month closer to 9:30, so a fixed time early
  enough for most Mondays would sometimes come before the counts, and one late enough to be safe
  would leave the list waiting on most Mondays.
- **credit:** full when the answer chooses the coordinator starting it once the counts are in
  (including saving the counts starting it, or a check every few minutes that starts it once the
  counts are there) and gives a reason tied to this workflow of any of the three kinds: what makes
  it good here, what would go wrong here without it, or what would make a specific other kind
  worse. The ruling is on whether the design would actually run once she is back and the counts
  are in, and give her the list when she wants it, not on whether the reason uses the word
  "available". One tied reason is enough, and a reason that also names what the choice costs still
  earns full credit. A reason resting on the irrelevant detail, on a fact with no bearing on the
  choice, or on a fact the scenario does not give (that she forgets, that a run is costly) is not
  tied. An answer listing two choices is ruled as choosing neither; a combined design is ruled by
  its main choice. Half when the answer chooses a fixed schedule with a reason resting on a fact
  that bears on the choice (for example, "a schedule at 8:45 Monday, because she is almost always
  back by 8:30"), or chooses the person with a generic, untied or missing reason. None when the
  choice is not accepted and the reason is generic, untied or missing, or no trigger is chosen.
- **tutor note:** the likely miss is a schedule at 8:45, read off "almost always between 8 and
  8:30"; that is half. After the ruling, ask what the agent has to work from on the Monday traffic
  holds her up.
