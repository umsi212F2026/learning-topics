Shape `event-best`, variant `told`, Medium. The facts favor an event clearly: a few bookings a day
at unpredictable times, a confirmation needed within the hour, a costly run, an always-on machine,
and a booking form that can send word.

Natural choice: an event, each new booking, noticed by being told by the booking form. Strongest
alternative: a schedule frequent enough to be timely, the agent run every fifteen or thirty minutes
on the front-desk computer.

For the event, against that schedule:

- Helps the cost of empty runs: "usually three or four a day, at any hour" and "it reads through
  every booking on our calendar ... which takes a few minutes and costs a little in agent fees, even
  when nothing is new." A run every fifteen minutes is about a hundred runs a day, nearly all of
  them empty and each paying the full cost; the event runs only the three or four times there is a
  booking.
- Also acceptable as the helped goal, timeliness: "Guests who don't hear from us within the hour
  tend to phone ... and some book somewhere else instead." Being told starts the run within moments
  of the booking, where a schedule makes a guest wait up to its interval.
- Hurts people's effort at set-up: "taking in those messages means my son setting something up on
  the front-desk computer, and he only comes by on Sundays and grumbles about computer chores." The
  schedule needs nothing from him.

For the schedule, against the event (half at most in this shape; see the activity's Credit): helps
people's effort at set-up (no message from the form to take in, so no son needed), from the same
sentence; hurts the cost of empty runs, from the same two sentences as above.

Noticing: being told fits, because the booking form can send a message to a web address each time
someone books, so there are no empty looks, and the hour's deadline is met the moment a booking
arrives. Checking repeatedly would also work but buys nothing here: the scenario describes no cheap
way to look, only the agent's costly read of the whole calendar.

Plainly weak options: a person, since bookings come at any hour and "I forget anything I'd have to
start by hand"; a schedule once or twice a day, which misses the hour for most guests.

When the machine is on: always ("The front-desk computer is never turned off"), so no option fails
on availability.

Irrelevant detail: "The confirmation email has a photo of our garden at the top." The room count is
background too and bears on no goal.

Generic reasons that fail: "events are faster", "a schedule is automatic", "being told is quicker
than checking", "polling wastes resources".

### q1

- **goal:** `c-choose-trigger`
- **cases:** event
- **answer:** An event, each new booking, noticed by being told by the booking form, since the form
  can send a message to a web address each time someone books. Compared with running the agent
  every fifteen or thirty minutes, it helps the cost of empty runs: there are only three or four
  bookings a day, and every run rereads the whole calendar and costs fees even when nothing is new
  (or it helps timeliness: guests phone or book elsewhere if they wait more than an hour). It hurts
  people's effort at set-up: taking in the form's messages needs the owner's son, who comes only on
  Sundays and grumbles about computer chores.
- **credit:** full for choosing an event; comparing it with a workable alternative (the frequent
  schedule, or another the key doesn't count as plainly weak); naming one goal it helps and one it
  hurts against that alternative, each with a reason tied to a fact in this scenario; and saying it
  should be noticed by being told, or by checking repeatedly, with a reason tied to this workflow
  (the form can send word, the hour's deadline, what each look costs). Cheap repeated looks that
  start the agent only when a booking is new count as the event noticed by checking repeatedly,
  whatever the answer calls them. Half when one goal reason is tied and the other generic or
  missing; when both are tied but the alternative is missing or plainly weak; when everything else
  is there but the noticing part is missing or untied; or when the answer chooses a person, or a
  schedule that runs the agent itself each time, however well tied its reasons (such an answer does
  not pass `event`). None when neither goal reason is tied, or no trigger is chosen. A reason
  resting on the garden photo is not tied.
- **tutor note:** if the answer says the event helps timeliness against a fifteen-minute schedule,
  that is tied (the hour's deadline) even though both would usually beat the hour; accept it. If
  the answer picks a frequent schedule because the son's set-up is a bother, give at most half,
  rule on the rest, and after the ruling ask how the workflow would notice a booking if a booking
  started it.
